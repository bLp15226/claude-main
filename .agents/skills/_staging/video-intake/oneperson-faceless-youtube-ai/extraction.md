## 1. Niche and format selection

**1.1 The "holy trinity of high-profit niches."** [01:15–01:33] Three conditions
together: videos **over 25 minutes**, a **Western audience**, viewers **over 40**.
Offered as the reason this category monetises well rather than merely gets views.
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE) — the mechanism is not stated but is worth naming,
> because it is the transferable part.** Each condition is an RPM lever, not a views
> lever: long runtime permits more mid-roll ad breaks per view; Western geography
> carries higher advertiser CPMs; older audiences are worth more per impression to
> advertisers. Read that way, the trinity is one principle — **optimise for revenue per
> view, not views** — and it is the same principle that makes Meta CPMs vary by geography
> and age. That generalisation is ours, not the speaker's, and it is the reason this
> item is dual-tagged to a paid-media reader as well as a channel one.

**1.2 "Niche quilting" — hold the format, transplant the market.** [02:09–02:52] His own

operator keeping the structure and changing the subject. His worked example: "10 US
Bread Brands to Avoid" → "10 US Cat Food Brands to Avoid," market chosen because he had
separately seen cat content perform. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

## 2. Production pipeline — the transferable mechanics

Stripped of tool specifics, the pipeline is: persistent niche context → title by
transplant → script with injected unique research → voice → visuals → thumbnail by
composition transfer → description. The tool-agnostic mechanics:

**2.1 Persistent per-channel project context.** [03:41–04:08] Create a dedicated AI
project per channel whose standing instructions carry the niche and the target
audience, so every later request inherits them. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS,
DEMONSTRATED.*

> Corroboration only, no proposal: this is architecturally what PHC's skill stack
> already does. Noted, not adopted.

**2.2 Style transfer by reference, not topic theft.** [05:02–05:19] When generating a
script, supply the **URL of a proven video** as a *style* reference — "it will model
their style of writing" — while the topic remains your own. *STATED, PLATFORM
MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

This is the cleanest statement in the video of the distinction that matters: **borrow
structure and register, not subject matter.** It aligns with Faber's existing
pattern-not-replica rule (see §5.2).

**2.3 De-genericisation by injected source material.** [04:33–04:59] Feed the generator
real outside material — PDFs found online, podcasts, other videos on the subject — as
context, "so that we're not sharing generic stuff like everyone else." Paired prompt:
*"Find five lesser-known facts and obscure details I can share in this video."*
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED / the prompt DEMONSTRATED.*

> **This is the video's most useful mechanic and also the site of its worst failure.**
> The technique — differentiate AI output by grounding it in specific outside sources —
> is sound and transfers to any AI-assisted content work. But the video attaches **no
> verification step whatsoever** between "AI found five obscure facts" and "publish a
> consumer-safety video naming real brands." See §6.2.

**2.4 Monetisation goal should shape the script.** [05:20–05:35] The generator asks what
the video monetises — ad revenue or a digital product — and produces a structurally
different script accordingly. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

> Worth keeping as a principle independent of any tool: **content built for reach and
> content built to convert are different artefacts, and the decision belongs upstream of
> the draft, not after it.** Directly relevant to organic content that carries a
> conversion goal.

**2.5 Thumbnail by composition transfer.** [10:32–11:24] Take a proven thumbnail, keep
its composition, and swap the subject for the equivalent object in your niche. His
selection criterion is **recognisability**, established by asking an LLM "what's the most
popular cat food brand in America that people would recognise on the thumbnail" →
Fancy Feast → "replace tuna can with a cat food spill from Fancy Feast."
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.*

The recognisability criterion is the genuinely useful half: the swapped object must be
instantly identifiable to the target viewer, or the composition's borrowed effectiveness
is lost. **The execution shown is rejected on two independent grounds** — it edits a
downloaded copy of the competitor's actual thumbnail image (§6.3), and it produces a
fabricated safety claim about a named product (§6.1).

**2.6 A free path exists at every paid step.** [05:51–06:01, 07:16–07:40, 09:12–09:40,
11:05–11:24] Stated repeatedly and unprompted: record your own voice with a free
recorder; edit manually in CapCut; generate the thumbnail in ChatGPT; do the visuals
yourself over "a couple of hours or two days," which is how he says he started.
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

> **Credibility note, in his favour.** For a video this heavily monetised, naming the
> free substitute at each step is a real disclosure, and he also **revises a vendor
> claim downward against his own interest** — the AI tool advertises 6 videos per $99
> tier and he says "from my calculations, it actually turns out to be around four."
> That is the opposite of the usual failure mode and is worth recording as calibration
> on the rest of his claims.

**2.7 Operational detail.** TTS input over 10,000 characters must be split into batches
and generated separately. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.* Tool-specific;
recorded only because it constrains how a long script is processed.

**2.8 Description for ranking.** [12:38–12:56] Generate the description from title plus
full script. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.* **Thin** — no ranking mechanics,
no keyword method, no evidence. Nothing adoptable beyond "write it from the script."

## 5. Relationship to existing PHC doctrine

**5.1 Direct conflict on method — PHC governs.** §2.3's research step, as practised here,
generates the factual content of a consumer-safety video from an AI prompt with no
verification gate. This contradicts PHC's Third Law (**Verified over plausible** — "no
state-laundering; a guess never gets written in a confident, declarative sentence").
Per Axis 1 this is a *process* claim, so neither the book stack nor recent-practitioner
authority governs it: **PHC's own operating rules do, and they win outright.** The
technique is adoptable only with a verification gate bolted on — which is a PHC
addition, not something this source supplies.

**5.2 Corroboration, and a violation, of the same Faber rule — inside one video.**
Verified against `faber-creative-flywheel/references/faber-video-karolis-ai-ugc.md` §6.3
and Faber's `SKILL.md` (rule promoted at v1.2.0, 2026-07-28: *"Analyse structure. Never
replicate a specific creator's execution beat-for-beat."*):

- **Corroborates it (third independent instance).** Niche quilting (§1.2) and style
  transfer (§2.2) are both explicit statements that copying a specific execution fails
  and transplanting a pattern works — and §1.3 supplies **frame-verified evidence** for
  it, which the two prior instances did not. This is the first source to bring data.
- **Violates it (§2.5).** The thumbnail method downloads the competitor's actual
  thumbnail image and edits it. That is replicating one identified creator's specific
  execution — precisely what the rule forbids — and it also has a copyright problem
  (§6.3).

**The tension is inside the source, not between the source and us.** Its title-level
advice complies with the rule; its thumbnail-level advice breaks it. Useful as a worked
example of where the line falls: **compose from the pattern, do not edit the artefact.**

**5.3 No conflict with Scriptor.** §3.2's offer statement and §1.2's negative-framing
title formula are consistent with existing offer and headline doctrine. Whether either
adds anything not already covered needs a coverage check before any proposal.

## 6. ETHICS FILTER — REJECTED TACTICS AND FLAGS

**6.1 Fabricated safety claim about a named real product. REJECTED — most serious item.**
Frame-verified at 11:03: the generated thumbnail reads **"POISON IN CAT FOOD"** in large
yellow type over a photorealistic image of a spilled can with the **Fancy Feast brand
name and trade dress clearly legible**. The claim is invented for click-through; nothing
anywhere in the video substantiates it, and the "research" behind the video is an
unverified AI prompt (§2.3).

This is a fabricated factual claim of consumer harm against an identifiable commercial
product. It carries product-disparagement and defamation exposure, plus platform
policy risk on medical/safety misinformation, and it violates PHC's honest-claims rule
outright. **Not adoptable in any form, at any scale, for any niche.** The *composition
transfer* mechanic (§2.5) survives only with truthful text.

**6.2 No verification gate in a pipeline producing factual claims. REJECTED as a method.**
The pipeline generates a consumer-warning video about named brands with zero fact-check
step between AI generation and publication. See §5.1. PHC's Third Law governs and the
video loses. **If any part of this pipeline is adopted, a verification gate is a
precondition, not an enhancement.**

**6.3 Editing a competitor's actual thumbnail image. REJECTED.** [11:05–11:24] The stated
method uses a "YouTube thumbnail grabber" to download the original thumbnail, then edits
that file. That is using someone else's copyrighted image as the base asset, and it
independently breaks Faber's pattern-not-replica rule (§5.2). Rebuilding a similar
*composition* from scratch is fine; editing their file is not.

**6.4 Selling one-click unverified AI output as a paid guide. REJECTED as described.**
§3.3: niche string plus channel URL → outline, full PDF, sales page, "build it for me."
Charging $47 for guidance with no subject-matter expertise or verification behind it is
incompatible with "feel like family, not a transaction." **The mechanic of attaching a
product to an audience is sound and kept (§3.1–3.2); this generation path is not.**

**6.5 Undisclosed synthetic content — unaddressed compliance gap.** The entire output is
AI voiceover plus AI visuals on a faceless channel, and the video never mentions
platform disclosure requirements for synthetic media. Flagged as an **open question**
rather than a rejected tactic — the omission may be an oversight rather than advice to
conceal, but anyone running this pipeline needs to resolve it.

**6.6 Undisclosed commercial interest — source-reliability caveat.** Solo appears at
nearly every step with an integration depth that implies ownership or a deep
affiliation, never disclosed; the second AI tool is gated behind his referral code
("that's how you get accepted instantly"); the free community is the funnel and the
lead magnets are its content. Not an ethics rejection — it is normal creator
monetisation — but **every tool-efficacy and pricing claim in this video carries a
conflict of interest and must travel with it.** Partly offset by §2.6's free-path
disclosures and the downward revision of a vendor's own claim.

**6.7 Misattributed origination — reliability flag, not a tactic.** See §1.3(b). The
central case study's framing is unsupported by the video's own screen. Bears on how much
weight the whole narrative carries, so it is recorded here rather than buried in §1.
