## Theme 1 — The zero-COGS offer and its unit economics

**T1.1 — The profitability rule for a zero-fulfilment-cost product is CPA < selling price.**
STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED. Because there is no cost of goods and no shipping,
the only variable cost per order is ad spend plus payment fees, so the break-even test
collapses to a single comparison: cost per purchase against the price tag ($24.99 here).
The speaker states this explicitly at 15:15 and repeats it at 17:29 as the sole metric he
watches across the test.

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE).** This is not a general rule — it is the specific case of a
> COGS-free product. PHC's catalogue is physical and landed-cost-bearing, so the same
> comparison run against a PHC product would overstate profitability by the entire COGS
> line. If any part of this file travels, this rule must travel with the condition attached.

**T1.2 — Digital front-end, physical back-end: the file gets them through the door, the
upsell earns the margin.** STATED · PERSUASION · ASSERTED. At 08:47 the speaker frames the
PDF as an entry offer and the post-purchase upsell as where profit is protected. The
complements were chosen for topical adjacency to the guide's problem (a ceiling projector
and a white-noise machine attached to a baby-sleep guide), sourced on AliExpress and
imported through AutoDS. One upsell converted across the run.

**T1.3 — Offer terms carried in the ad itself.** STATED · PERSUASION · DEMONSTRATED per
narration. The closing beat of the ad names all four commercial terms in one breath: price
($24), delivery mechanism (instant download), risk reversal (30-day guarantee), and CTA
(link below). Nothing is held back for the landing page.

## Theme 5 — Creative: sourcing, structure, production

**T5.1 — Search the problem in the customer's own words to find organic formats that already
work.** STATED · PERSUASION · ASSERTED [11:01]. He searches "get baby to sleep" on TikTok and
reads the result set for a repeatable shape: a parent on camera, the problem stated as lived
experience, a solution promoted, a soft CTA. He also notices the top creator is selling her
own course into the same audience — a demand signal read off the search page itself.

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE).** The *reading* here is legitimate research and survives the
> ethics filter intact. What he does with the videos afterwards does not. These are separable
> and must be kept separate in anything that travels: search-as-format-research is adoptable;
> the download step is not.

**T5.2 — Ad script structure, as delivered [13:10–13:29].** STATED · PERSUASION ·
DEMONSTRATED per narration. Five beats, ~20 seconds:
1. **Conditional identification** — "if bedtime with your baby feels like clocking into a
   second job, this is for you." Names the felt experience, not the demographic.
2. **The scene, timestamped** — "you worked all day and now it's 7:00 p.m." A specific hour
   rather than a general complaint; the viewer places themselves in it.
3. **The ritual, itemized** — "two hours rocking, feeding, and praying to a crib." Three
   concrete actions; the third carries the joke and the exhaustion at once.
4. **Mechanism with a number and a timeframe** — "there's a 14-night plan that teaches your
   baby to fall asleep on their own." Names what it is and how long it takes.
5. **Terms and exit** — "$24, instant download, 30-day guarantee, link below, go to bed."
   The sign-off doubles as a benefit restatement.

**T5.3 — Production chain for the presenter clip.** STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED
[12:11–13:06]. Pinterest search for a reference photo ("mother holding baby selfie") → model
generates a similar figure → script split by line → each line animated as a separate ~10s
720p clip at a named image-to-video tool (transcript renders it "clean 3.0 turbo"; **the tool
name is unverified from transcript alone** — do not repeat it as fact) → a human editor
assembles the clips. **The presenter is synthetic and the reference was a real person's
photo — see the ethics filter.**

**T5.4 — The quality bar applied was "good enough to test," stated openly.** STATED ·
PERSUASION · ASSERTED [13:34]. *"It's not the highest quality video, but … this is good
enough to toss into the campaign."* Consistent with treating test creative as disposable.

## Ethics filter — REJECTED

**E1 — Downloading other creators' TikTok videos, stripping the watermark, and running them
as paid ads.** [11:43] Two of the three creatives were obtained this way. The speaker names
the problem himself and routes around it rather than resolving it: *"I don't want to infringe
on her copyright, but uh you got to read between the lines a bit here."* **Rejected: this is
knowing use of another creator's work as commercial advertising without licence, with the
attribution mark deliberately removed.** The watermark removal is what converts a grey
practice into a deliberate one — it exists only to hide the source. It also means two thirds
of the reported result rests on assets PHC could not lawfully have run, which is a second,
independent reason the numbers do not transfer.

**E2 — A synthetic presenter generated from a real person's photograph.** [12:11] The
reference image was a real mother found via Pinterest search; the ad figure was generated to
resemble her. **Rejected on two grounds:** the likeness of an identifiable person was used
commercially without consent, and Faber's standing rule already refuses a fabricated
presenter outright (`faber-creative-flywheel/references/brief-templates.md:124`; `SKILL.md`
lines 145–148). Noting a genuine boundary detail for the ruling rather than smoothing it
over: **the script as delivered is a spokesperson read, not a testimonial** — the figure says
"this is for you," never "this worked for me." That distinction matters elsewhere but does
not rescue this instance, because the likeness problem is independent of the testimony
question and Faber's rule bans the fabricated presenter as such.

**E3 — Selling unread AI-generated infant-sleep advice.** [03:58] A 32-page guide instructing
parents on getting babies to sleep through the night, sold at $24.99, which the seller states
he did not read. **Rejected.** Infant sleep is health-adjacent and safety-adjacent — sleep
positioning, night-weaning and cry-it-out protocols are exactly the content where wrong
advice reaches a baby. This is not a copy-integrity objection, it is a product-integrity one:
the seller cannot stand behind a claim in his own product. PHC's brand ethos is "feel like
family, not a transaction," and this is its inverse.

**E4 — The 30-day guarantee on an instantly-downloaded file.** [13:26] Not rejected, flagged.
A refund promise on a non-returnable digital good is legitimate only if actually honoured
without friction; the video never returns to it. Stated as offered, unverified in practice.

**Nothing else tripped the filter.** Notably absent from this source and worth recording as a
negative finding: no countdown timer, no fake stock counter, no invented review, no fabricated
price anchor, no manufactured urgency of any kind. The ad sells on the problem and the terms.

## Conflicts with existing doctrine

**C1 — Same-day scaling.** Video: double the daily budget after one day and three purchases
[16:00]. Doctrine: *"Never classify, diagnose, or scale off a single day"*
(`legatus-meta-ads/references/diagnostic-taxonomy.md:324`), plus a 7-day management cadence
and a defined scaling increment applied no more than once per stated interval
(`SKILL.md:258`). **Stronger basis: PHC doctrine.** This is a PLATFORM-MECHANICS claim, where
recent practitioner content would normally lead — but the video offers no evidence for the
decision beyond one day's margin on eight lifetime orders, while Legatus's rule is a
sample-size argument that holds on any platform. The video's own data cannot distinguish its
scale-up from noise. Note the scope difference honestly: Legatus is Meta-scoped and this is
TikTok, so the *cadence numbers* may not transfer — but "not off one day" is not a
Meta-specific claim.

**C2 — AI presenter.** Video treats a generated spokesperson as routine. Faber refuses a
fabricated presenter (`brief-templates.md:124`; `SKILL.md:145–148`), and has already
adjudicated two prior sources on this exact point (Karolis 2026-07-22, Blackie). **Stronger
basis: PHC doctrine, already settled twice.** No re-litigation proposed.

**C3 — Creative sourcing.** Video downloads competitors' finished videos. Faber's existing
line is *"several performers in, an original build out"* — pattern, not replica
(`brief-templates.md:132–138`). **Stronger basis: PHC doctrine.** The video's *search* method
(T5.1) is compatible with that line; its *download* step is the exact thing the line exists
to prohibit.
