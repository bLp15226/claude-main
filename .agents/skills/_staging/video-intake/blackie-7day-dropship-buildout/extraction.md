## Theme 1 — Product selection and validation

**1.1 The stated selection filter.** Products that (a) solve a problem, (b) are unique,
(c) are already viral, and (d) target what he calls "Facebook moms," a demographic he
characterises as the easiest to sell to. *STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE · ASSERTED.*

> **PHC synthesis — the filter did not produce his winner.** His own rule leads with "solves
> a problem," and he applied it to the electric razor, which **failed**. The product that
> worked — a fill-in-the-blank memoir book for a parent — solves no problem at all; it is a
> gift/identity purchase on an emotional angle. The video never notices this. The transferable
> lesson is the opposite of the stated rule: *the emotional-gift category outperformed the
> problem-solver here.* This is INFERENCE, drawn across the two tests, not his claim.

**1.2 Spy tools are a crowded input, not an edge.** He found the failed razor on PP Ads and,
post-mortem, argues the weakness is structural rather than product-specific: a spy tool surfaces
the same product to everyone subscribed, so perhaps a thousand operators receive the same signal
simultaneously. He prefers scroll-discovery on his own Facebook/Instagram feeds.
*STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED.* Evidence is one failed test — the reasoning is
plausible but the video does not establish it.

**1.3 The validation method — the most transferable mechanic in the video.** A three-step check,
run before any spend:

1. **Find a *sponsored* post with organic-scale engagement in the wild.** Not an organic post —
   a live paid ad carrying a Shop Now button, with engagement far past what a cold ad normally
   accrues (he cites ~22k likes / ~1k comments / ~5k shares). Engagement at that level on a paid
   placement implies the ad has been running long enough and well enough to accumulate it.
2. **Confirm in the Meta Ad Library** that the same advertiser has ads live *for months*, not days.
3. **Check creative volume and variety** in the Library — many distinct creatives means the
   advertiser is actively testing and scaling, which is spend they would not commit to a loser.

Longevity plus creative volume is the inference engine: nobody funds months of varied creative on
an unprofitable product. *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED (reasoning), DEMONSTRATED PER
NARRATION (the Library view itself).*

**1.4 Competitor-weakness check.** He additionally screens for whether existing competitor ads are
*poor* — a proxy for "there is room to do it better." Notably this check passed on the razor,
which still failed. *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED.*

## Theme 2 — Test campaign structure and the judgment window

**2.1 Test structure.** One campaign → **one ad set → five creatives inside it**, at a ~$50/day
budget (he used $51.77 and, later, $100.77; the odd cents are never explained and appear to be a
personal tic, not a mechanic). No audience splitting, no multi-ad-set matrix — the creatives
compete inside a single ad set and Meta arbitrates. *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED.*

**2.2 The three-day window before any verdict.** Day 1 losing money is treated as expected, not as
a signal. His narration of the winning product's arc:

| Day | Orders | Revenue | His read | Re-derived |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | $79.96 | "not profitable" | correct — ROAS 1.14 on $70 spend, under 1.46 break-even |
| 2 | 4 | $159.92 | "probably break even" | **wrong — profitable.** ROAS 2.28–3.08 depending on actual spend, well over break-even |
| 3 | 5 | $199.90 | "definitely profitable" | correct — ROAS 3.88 on $51.46 |
| 4 (challenge d7) | 7 | $279.86 | winner | ROAS ~3.2 on ~$87 |

*DEMONSTRATED PER NARRATION · PERFORMANCE CLAIM.* He flags his own day-2 read as unrounded
("I haven't really run the numbers yet"), so this is a self-disclosed soft spot rather than a
misrepresentation. **PHC note:** the campaign turned profitable a day earlier than his own
narrative claims, which if anything strengthens the case for the patience rule.

**2.3 Do not touch it during learning.** Explicit: he changes nothing on day 1 despite a $35 CPA,
reasoning that Meta is still resolving which creative earns the cheapest conversions and that
edits "confuse it." *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED.* Consistent with existing Legatus
learning-phase doctrine — no conflict.

**2.4 Meta overspends the daily budget.** He reports $70 spent against a $51.77 daily budget and
jokes about "Zuck stealing an extra $20." *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · DEMONSTRATED PER
NARRATION.*

> **PHC synthesis — do not adopt this figure.** $70 on $51.77 is **35.2% over budget**. Meta's
> documented behaviour permits up to **25%** daily overspend, rebalanced across the calendar week.
> 35% exceeds the documented ceiling, so one of the following is true: his number is rounded/loose,
> the ad set ran longer than the 24h he describes, or the budget was not what he stated. The
> *phenomenon* (daily budgets overshoot; plan cash against ~1.25×, not 1.0×) is real and worth
> holding. The *magnitude* here is unverified. INFERENCE.

## Theme 3 — Unit economics and the scale trigger

**3.1 Break-even ROAS formula.** Sale price ÷ (sale price − landed product cost).
Worked: $39.98 ÷ ($39.98 − $12.54) = $39.98 ÷ $27.44 = **1.457**, which he states as 1.46.
*STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · CLAIMED.* **Arithmetic re-derived and correct.**

> **PHC synthesis — the formula is right but incomplete.** It nets only COGS + inbound shipping.
> It excludes payment processing (~2.9% + $0.30 ≈ $1.46 on a $39.98 order), refunds/chargebacks,
> and any app/platform cost. Folding processing alone in moves true break-even from 1.46 to
> roughly **1.54**. On a thin margin that gap is the difference between scaling a winner and
> scaling a break-even product. If PHC adopts the formula, adopt it with the fee line included.

**3.2 The scale trigger.** When measured ROAS materially clears break-even ROAS, raise budget.
Executed: day-3 ROAS 3.88 vs. 1.46 break-even → budget $51.77 → **$100.77, a 1.95× jump in a
single step.** *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · DEMONSTRATED PER NARRATION.*

> **CONFLICT WITH EXISTING DOCTRINE — flagged, not resolved.** Legatus
> (`references/diagnostic-taxonomy.md`, "Scaling math") mandates increments of **1.2×–1.5×**,
> prefers **1.2× for a first scale**, and caps frequency at once per 3 days, on the reasoning that
> large jumps buy a slug of slow-converting top-funnel impressions and produce the post-scale dip
> that gets healthy campaigns killed. Blackie's 1.95× is ~30% above Legatus's ceiling and is a
> first scale, where Legatus wants the low end.
>
> **Which source has the stronger basis for this claim type?** Honestly: **neither is strong.**
> Per Axis 1 this is PLATFORM MECHANICS, where recent practitioner content is the primary
> authority and the book stack is irrelevant — but Legatus's 1.2×–1.5× range is *self-labelled in
> its own reference file* as "sourced from the same general media-buying practice as the KPI
> placeholders above, **not PHC-specific data**." So this is practitioner-vs-practitioner, both at
> ASSERTED tier. The video's counter-evidence is n=1 and weak: his campaign did **not** visibly
> break after the 1.95× jump (day 4 held ~3.2 ROAS), but the video ends immediately after, so no
> post-scale observation window exists. **Recommend Ben rule on this explicitly rather than either
> side auto-winning.**

**3.3 Price architecture.** $29.99 headline price + $9.99 shipping charged at checkout = **$39.98
effective**. The margin math is run on $39.98, not $29.99. *STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE ·
ASSERTED.* PHC note: split pricing improves the ad-to-page price impression but is exactly the
pattern Centurion's checkout-integrity lane exists to watch — a shipping charge that surprises at
checkout is a documented conversion leak, and it collides with a "feel like family, not a
transaction" brand ethos. Adopt only with the shipping cost disclosed pre-checkout.

**3.4 Private supplier over public marketplace.** He uses HyperSKU as a private supplier: send a
screenshot of the product being sold, the supplier sources and links it to Shopify; orders flow to
their dashboard, he pays, they ship direct. Landed cost broken out as **$3.36 product + $9.18
shipping = $12.54**, at 5–9 day delivery. Claims private supply is both cheaper and better quality
than public options. *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · DEMONSTRATED PER NARRATION (the dashboard) +
CLAIMED (the cheaper/better assertion, which is unevidenced).*

## Theme 4 — Creative, and the hook-rate mechanic

**4.1 Hook rate is the creative arbitration metric.** The strongest single finding in the video.
Two creatives inside the same ad set:

| Creative | Hook rate | Spend | Sales | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early leader | 35% | $9 (stalled) | 1 (at $1 CPA, day 1) | starved by the algorithm |
| Eventual winner | 75% | $87 | 7 | absorbed nearly all budget, 3.2 ROAS |

Meta consolidated spend onto the higher hook-rate creative and effectively defunded the other —
*including one that had produced a $1 CPA sale on day 1.* *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS ·
DEMONSTRATED PER NARRATION.*

> **PHC synthesis — two distinct lessons here, and the second is the valuable one.**
> (a) Hook rate is a leading indicator of which creative the algorithm will fund; 75% is
> characterised as very high, 35% as weak. (b) **A spectacular early CPA on tiny spend is noise
> the algorithm will correctly ignore.** The $1 CPA creative was the single best-looking line item
> on day 1 and it was *right* to defund it — $1 of spend carries no information. This is a direct
> caution against PHC reading a low-spend outlier as a winner. INFERENCE, drawn across his day-1
> and day-7 readouts.
>
> **Coverage note:** "hook rate" appears **nowhere** in the current skill tree (grepped
> 2026-07-28, zero hits across all installed skills). This is genuinely new vocabulary for PHC,
> not a restatement.

**4.2 The three-beat creative structure.** Built in CapCut, five variants per test:

1. **Beat 1 (0–3s) — a broad, unresolved first-person hook.** His: a stated regret about not
   having given the product to his father before he died. His own rationale for why it works:
   it is *very broad*, and almost anyone reading it wants to know what happened — the statement
   raises questions it does not answer, so the viewer stays to resolve them.
2. **Beat 2 (3–6s) — a relatability beat.** A second person, in a comparable situation
   (a funeral, a stated bereavement), narrowing from "anyone" to "the exact customer."
3. **Beat 3 — product introduced as the resolution** to the emotional state beats 1–2 created.

*STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE · ASSERTED.* The structure — curiosity gap → identification →
solution — is orthodox direct response and survives the ethics filter intact. **The execution does
not; see Theme 5.**

**4.3 Emotional angle over feature angle.** For a product with no functional problem to solve,
the entire creative load is carried by emotional positioning rather than demonstration or benefit.
*STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE · ASSERTED.* See the §1.1 synthesis — this, not his stated filter,
is what actually worked.

## Theme 5 — REJECTED TACTICS (ethics filter, PHC standing rules)

Each rejected with its reason. Never folded into doctrine above.

**5.1 REJECTED — "heaps of social proof as usual" on a store with zero customers.**
He states the product page is finished with substantial social proof and that social proof "is
really all that matters on a product page." The store is **days old and has made zero sales** at
that point. There is no authentic source for reviews on a store with no customers; the proof is
necessarily imported, purchased, or invented.
**Reason for rejection:** collides head-on with Scriptor's standing rule #1 — *no fabricated
reviews or borrowed testimonials, zero permitted inventory until Founding 50 produces real,
attributed ones* — and with PHC's own fake-review-wipe history, which is why that rule exists.
Also exposure under the FTC Consumer Reviews Rule. **Do not adopt in any form.** Note that the
*claim* underneath ("social proof is the highest-leverage element on a product page") may well be
true; PHC's constraint is that it must be earned first.

**5.2 REJECTED — fabricated bereavement testimony as the creative's proof.**
The winning creative is built on first-person grief presented as real: a stated personal loss of a
father, plus a second person at a funeral stating she lost her father two years ago. Nothing
indicates these are the creator's own experience or a real customer's; this is a four-day-old
dropshipping store.
**Reason for rejection:** manufactured emotional proof — a fabricated testimonial in the most
load-bearing position in the ad. Faber's hard refusal on fabricated testimonials and proof
(explicitly including AI-generated UGC) covers exactly this.
**Separable:** the three-beat *structure* in §4.2 is adoptable. What is rejected is populating it
with invented personal testimony. PHC can run curiosity-gap → identification → solution using
genuine founder voice or genuine customer words.

**5.3 NOT rejected — the mortality framing itself (flagged for Ben, not a violation).**
The angle "give this to your parent before they pass away" is emotionally aggressive but rests on
a **real reason-why** — mortality is genuine, not a manufactured deadline. It therefore passes
PHC's fake-urgency/fake-scarcity test cleanly and should not be logged as an ethics rejection.
It is a **brand-fit question**: whether grief-leverage marketing is compatible with "premium,
family-first, feel like family, not a transaction." That is Ben's call, not the filter's.

**5.4 FLAGGED, not rejected — the targeting rationale.**
"Facebook moms… because they are the easiest to sell to" frames audience selection around a
demographic's low sales resistance rather than around fit between product and need. Not a banned
tactic and not an ethics rejection. Flagged because the *framing* is the transactional posture
PHC's brand ethos explicitly rejects, and because — per §1.1 — the demographic heuristic is not
what actually produced his winner.

## Theme 6 — Performance claims (all provisional; PHC data governs)

Every number below is CLAIMED or DEMONSTRATED PER NARRATION. None is verified. Per Axis 1 these
stay provisional until PHC's own account confirms or kills them, and **no threshold in any skill
should be set from them.**

**6.1 Headline result.** 18 orders / **$719.64** revenue across the 7-day challenge (he states
"$719"). Re-derived: 18 × $39.98 = $719.64. ✓

**6.2 The order counts reconcile exactly, which pins the timeline.** 2 + 4 + 5 + 7 = 18, and
$79.96 + $159.92 + $199.90 + $279.86 = $719.64. Both reconcile with zero remainder, so the winning
product ran **exactly four days** with no unshown intervening days — the failed razor test consumed
the first ~3 days of the challenge. This matters: it closes off the possibility of hidden
zero-order days silently absorbing budget. *PHC synthesis, INFERENCE — but arithmetically forced.*

**6.3 What the headline number is not.** $719.64 is **revenue**, not profit, and the video never
nets ad spend against it.

- Gross profit before ad spend: 18 × $27.44 = **$493.92**
- Ad spend, reconstructed (two shown, two at budget-floor, plus the failed razor test):
  ~$52 (razor) + $70 + ~$52 + $51.46 + ~$100 = **$325 floor / ~$362 if Meta overspent on the
  unshown days as it did on day 1**
- **Net ≈ $131 – $168 over seven days.**

> **PHC synthesis — the honest read, and it is NOT "he hid a loss."** He was genuinely net
> positive. But true net is roughly **19–24% of the headline number he leads with**, and the
> video's closing beat — "$719, payouts already coming in, looking nice" — invites the viewer to
> read revenue as the result. The failed first test's spend is never subtracted anywhere. **The
> method is not discredited by this; the framing is what needs discounting.** INFERENCE
> throughout: days 2 and the razor test's actual spend were never shown, so the floor uses stated
> budgets. Stated as a reconstruction, not a fact.

**6.4 Other stated figures.** Failed razor test: ~$2 CPC, zero sales, ~24h. Day-1 winner: $35 CPA.
Day-3: $10.29 CPA, 3.88 ROAS. Winning creative lifetime: $87 spend, 7 sales, $12.43 CPA, 3.2 ROAS.
Unit margin $27.44. Landed cost $12.54 at 5–9 day shipping. *All CLAIMED or DEMONSTRATED PER
NARRATION.*

**6.5 The pitch figure — hold at arm's length.** He references a store he "took to a million
dollars in revenue in about 6 months," offered as the basis for a free case-study video in his
Discord. Wholly unverifiable, and it is the promotional claim in the video. *CLAIMED.* Per the
standing rule this does not discount the mechanics above — but it becomes no part of any doctrine.
