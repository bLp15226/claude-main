## 1. The competitor store read — method, with its own failure modes

**1.1 What a spy-tool store read yields.** [02:53–04:11] Paste a competitor URL and read:
estimated monthly revenue, monthly visits, geography, average visit duration, pages per
visit, traffic-source split, mobile/desktop split, top keywords, similar stores.
Frame-verified at 03:40 — header **Barbaradever**, **$1.2M/mo**, **459k** visits, plus a
health-score breakdown, a six-month traffic trend, and readouts of **3:41** average
duration, **4.5** pages per visit, and a fourth figure of **42%**.
*STATED, PERFORMANCE, DEMONSTRATED-as-estimate.*

Signals worth reading in PHC's own competitive work, in rough order of reliability:
mobile/desktop split (directly actionable — it tells you which viewport to audit in),
geography, pages per visit and visit duration as engagement proxies, traffic-source
mix, then revenue estimate last and least.

**1.2 Two calibration warnings on the tool itself.**

*(a) Conflict of interest.* The tool is **the speaker's own product**, and he opens by
saying he built it because rival tools are "wildly inaccurate." Its outputs are
estimates, not account data, and the person vouching for its accuracy sells it. The
revenue figure stays **CLAIMED**, never demonstrated, regardless of how it renders on
a dashboard.

*(b) A number I could not reconcile, and am not asserting a discrepancy over.* He
narrates "48% of their traffic was direct," while the on-screen figure adjacent to
duration and pages-per-visit reads **42%**. The traffic-source axis labels were not
legible at the 480p ceiling. These are plausibly two different metrics — bounce rate
versus direct share — rather than a contradiction. **Recorded as unverified in both
directions.**

**1.3 The direct-traffic inference — carried WITH its objection.**
He reads a high *direct* traffic share as evidence of ad clicks ("someone clicked on
the link from their ad"), then chains: heavy traffic → running ads → making money.
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

> **OBJECTION (PHC synthesis, INFERENCE) — this inference is unreliable and Augur
> should carry the objection, not the claim.**
>
> 1. **The attribution step is probably wrong.** Paid clicks normally carry a referrer
>    or UTM and land in the paid or social bucket. "Direct" is the bucket for traffic
>    with a *missing* referrer — app webviews, some in-app browsers, dark social,
>    manual entry, stripped referrers. A high direct share is as consistent with
>    messy measurement as with ad clicks.
> 2. **The profitability step is worse.** "Traffic implies ads implies money" omits CAC
>    entirely. A store can buy heavy traffic and lose money on every single order.
>    Volume is not evidence of unit economics, and a teardown that infers profit from
>    traffic is inferring the one thing the data cannot show.
>
> **Usable as:** a rough presence-of-spend signal — this store is probably buying
> traffic. **Worthless as:** evidence the store is profitable, or that its offer works.
> Any PHC competitive read that concludes "they're making money" from traffic alone
> has skipped the only step that mattered.

**1.4 Method note carried from Scriptor's file.** The size-chart gap in that teardown was
found by **competitor-convention scanning** — auditing many rivals and noticing a
feature *all* of them carried. Worth naming as a research technique in its own right:
convention gaps are visible only in aggregate, never from one competitor.

## 3. Dupe arbitrage and the saturation model

**Ruled to Augur for now, revisit for Beowulf if that general is built.**

**3.1 The arbitrage.** [18:44–19:20, 25:48–27:22] An expensive brand releases a garment
that becomes desirable; factories produce a knockoff; resellers sell it at a fraction,
and the buyer experiences a reference-price gap — "I saw that dress. I thought it was
$500. Here's where I can get it for $70." The value story is borrowed from the
original's price, not built from the product's own merits.
*STATED, PERSUASION + PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

**3.2 The copycat cascade, and why it is the real finding.** [24:06–25:47] Three further
stores are shown selling the identical garment — same AI images, same Impulse theme,
same page formula — one of them having "literally just ripped" the store he had spent
twenty minutes analysing. *STATED, DEMONSTRATED.*

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE) — read this as a market-structure warning, not a tactic.**
> In a category where the product is a commodity available to every reseller from the
> same aggregator, a demonstrated winner is copied within weeks. That is why the
> speaker's own winning-product test (§ Scriptor file 3.2) puts **saturation ahead of
> quality** as the disqualifier: quality is not defensible when the SKU is public.
>
> The competitive moat in such a category cannot be the product. It has to be brand,
> proof, service, or fulfilment speed — none of which a copycat inherits by importing
> your catalogue. This is the strategic argument *for* PHC's premium, family-first
> positioning rather than an argument against it: positioning is the part that cannot
> be ripped.

**3.3 A research signal that falls out of this.** Finding several stores selling one SKU
with identical imagery is a **saturation reading**, and a fast one. If PHC's product
research surfaces a candidate already carried by multiple stores with shared supplier
photography, the window on that item has likely closed.

## 4. RISK NOTE — legal exposure found during market intelligence

**Ruled: file as a risk note attached to Augur — market intelligence should flag legal
exposure it finds, not just opportunity. A note, not a doctrine rule.** Praetorian
stays unbuilt, consistent with the earlier ruling.

The source's business model carries exposure it never acknowledges:

- **Design-right / trade-dress exposure.** Knowingly selling factory knockoffs of a
  designer original. The video treats the original's desirability as the *reason* to
  sell the copy.
- **Copyright in imagery.** The speaker notes in passing that one competitor "stole
  these images from a different brand," and that suppliers lift product photography
  wholesale from the stores they supply.
- **Third-party video used as ad creative.** Harvesting existing TikTok videos of a
  product as creative raw material, with no mention of permission or licence. Ruled
  **rejected, not staged as doctrine** (ruling item 10) and logged as an open question
  only.

**Standing note for Augur's use:** when a competitive read turns up a model that only
works because someone else's design, photography, or footage is being used without
permission, that is a finding about the *durability* of the opportunity, not a
neutral detail. Record it alongside the opportunity.
