## 1 — The three-step validation check

Run before any spend. This is the most transferable mechanic in the video.

**Step 1 — Find a *sponsored* post carrying organic-scale engagement.**
Not an organic post. A live paid placement with a Shop Now button, showing engagement far past
what a cold ad normally accrues. His example: roughly 22k likes, 1k comments, 5k shares on a
sponsored unit. *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · DEMONSTRATED PER NARRATION (the post itself).*

The inference: a paid unit only accumulates engagement at that scale if it has been running long
enough and well enough to earn continued funding. Engagement on a *sponsored* placement is
therefore a proxy for sustained profitable spend in a way engagement on an organic post is not.

**Step 2 — Confirm longevity in the Meta Ad Library.**
Look up the advertiser and check that ads have been live **for months, not days**.
*STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · DEMONSTRATED PER NARRATION.*

**Step 3 — Check creative volume and variety.**
Many distinct creatives in the Library means the advertiser is actively testing and scaling.
*STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED.*

**The engine underneath all three:** nobody funds months of varied creative on an unprofitable
product. Longevity plus creative volume is a spend-revealed-preference signal — it reads someone
else's willingness to keep paying as evidence of demand. *PHC synthesis, INFERENCE.*

> **Limits worth stating.** This validates that a product **sells for someone**, not that it
> sells for PHC, at PHC's price point, to PHC's avatar, with PHC's creative. It is a demand
> filter, not a fit filter. It also carries a structural bias toward products already being
> worked by capable operators — which is the point when validating, and a liability when
> differentiating.

## 2 — Spy tools as a crowded input

He found his **failed** product on a paid spy tool (PP Ads) and, post-mortem, argues the weakness
is structural rather than product-specific: a spy tool surfaces the same product to every
subscriber simultaneously, so perhaps a thousand operators receive the same signal at once. He
prefers scroll-discovery in his own Facebook/Instagram feeds, then Library confirmation.
*STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED.*

**Evidence is one failed test.** The reasoning is plausible and worth holding as a hypothesis;
the video does not establish it. A single failure is equally consistent with an ordinary bad
product pick. Do not treat "spy tools are saturated" as settled.

**The transferable half is stronger than the claim:** the *feed-then-Library* sequence uses an
input (your own algorithmically-personalised feed) that is not identically distributed to every
competitor, which is a real structural difference regardless of whether spy tools are actually
saturated.

## 3 — The stated selection filter, and why it is the weakest part

**3.1 What he says he screens for:** products that (a) solve a problem, (b) are unique, (c) are
already viral, (d) target what he calls "Facebook moms," a demographic he characterises as the
easiest to sell to. *STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE · ASSERTED.*

**3.2 He also screens for competitor weakness** — whether existing competitor ads are poor, as a
proxy for room to do better. *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED.* Note this check **passed
on the product that failed.**

**3.3 PHC synthesis — the filter did not produce his winner. INFERENCE, drawn across both tests.**

His rule leads with "solves a problem," and he applied it to an electric razor, which **failed
outright** — zero sales, ~$2 CPC. The product that worked was a fill-in-the-blank memoir book
for a parent: it solves no problem at all. It is a gift and identity purchase carried entirely
by emotional positioning.

**The video never notices this.** The observed lesson runs opposite to the stated rule: in this
sample the emotional-gift category outperformed the problem-solver. That is n=1 on each side and
proves nothing on its own — but it is a direct caution against adopting "must solve a problem" as
a screening criterion on this source's authority, since the source's own evidence contradicts it.

**3.4 The demographic heuristic — flagged, not adopted.** "Facebook moms… because they are the
easiest to sell to" frames audience selection around a demographic's low sales resistance rather
than around fit between product and need. Not an ethics violation and not a banned tactic. Flagged
because the framing is the transactional posture PHC's brand ethos explicitly rejects
("feel like family, not a transaction"), and because per §3.3 the heuristic is not what actually
produced his winner. Augur's avatar work should continue to be need-and-language-driven, not
resistance-driven.

### The decline, with reasoning — recorded per the drift-log rule

> **Aurelius, 2026-07-28:** *"Blackie's own data contradicts his stated filter: the winning product solved
> nothing, an emotional gift item, while the filter he claims to use produced the failure. Don't adopt a
> screening heuristic that the source's own case study disproves."*

Two items declined:

1. **The "solves a problem" screen.** He states he only looks for products that solve a problem, applied it
   to the electric razor, and the razor **failed outright** — zero sales at ~$2 CPC. The product that worked
   solves no problem at all. **A screening heuristic contradicted by its own case study is not adopted**,
   and the video never notices the contradiction.
2. **The demographic-resistance targeting rationale** ("Facebook moms… because they are the easiest to sell
   to"). Selects an audience by low sales resistance rather than by fit between product and need. Not an
   ethics violation and not a banned tactic — declined because the framing is the transactional posture
   PHC's ethos rejects ("feel like family, not a transaction"), and because it is not what produced his
   winner either. **Augur's avatar work stays need-and-language-driven, not resistance-driven.**

Note this decline sits alongside **A-1, which was applied**: the Ad Library *validation* method from the
same source is adopted, his *selection filter* is not. Both judgments come from the same evidence — his
razor test failed and his memoir-book test worked — and the split is deliberate rather than inconsistent.
