## 1. Proof before spend — an extension, not a new principle

**Ruled a split: the principle came here; the execution mechanic (batch scheduling) went to
Herald. Augur owns what a sale licenses you to conclude; Herald owns how the content ships.**

**1.1 The claim.** [13:04–13:29] Everything upstream of a real purchase may be wrong:
*"Everything that we built up to this point could still be lying to us. The product looks
right, the page reads right, and the data said yes. But **none of it is proof until strangers
show real buying intent**."* *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

**1.2 The proof hierarchy, which is the quotable part.**
**"Clicks can lie all day. Compliments definitely lie, but purchases they never do."**
And: **"Sales are a bonus. Proof is the purchase."** The validation stage exists to answer
exactly one question — will strangers move on this product — and it is answered **without
spending anything on ads.** *STATED, PERSUASION, ASSERTED.*

**1.3 Why an organic purchase proves more than a paid one.** [17:31–17:43] A purchase from a
free organic post *"is actually telling you something that no ad click ever can"* — that
demand exists without having been bought. **"Organic buys the proof. Profit buys the speed."**
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

**1.4 Coverage — PARTIALLY COVERED, and the gap is a real one.**

> **Checked 2026-07-29** (searched: "before ad spend", "organic before paid", "kill before",
> "cheap truth"). `augur-doctrine-customer-conversations.md:147` **already gives Augur
> kill-switch authority before ad spend**:
>
> *"if a product's clean-signal report shows no unprompted problem mentions, no prior-solution
> attempts, and no commitment at Founding-50 pricing, Augur recommends kill before ad spend.
> **Cheap truth beats expensive hope** — that's the whole doctrine in six words."*
>
> So **PHC already holds the principle.** What PHC's version uses as its *instrument* is
> customer conversations plus a pricing commitment. This source supplies a **second,
> harder-edged instrument for the same gate: a completed purchase by a stranger, from a free
> post.** That is an **EXTENSION** — a new instrument, not a new doctrine.
>
> Also distinct from Centurion's pre-launch checklist, which also runs "before ad spend"
> (`store-health-methods.md:39`) but on an entirely different axis: functional integrity, not
> demand validation. Three gates, three axes, no conflict.

**1.5 PHC synthesis (INFERENCE) — where the instruments rank, and why it matters.**
Augur's existing instrument and this one are not interchangeable, and the ordering is the
useful addition:

| Instrument | What it costs | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| Unprompted problem mentions in conversation | Time | The problem is real |
| Commitment at Founding-50 pricing | Time, plus a soft ask | Someone says they would pay |
| **A completed purchase from a free organic post** | Time, plus content | **Someone did pay, unpurchased** |

The third is strictly stronger evidence and strictly slower to obtain. So it is not a
replacement for the kill-switch — it is the gate you use when a candidate **survives** the
conversation gate and you are deciding whether to spend. Stated as a rule: *the conversation
gate kills cheaply; the organic-purchase gate authorises spending.*

**1.6 The provisional claim under it.** *"I've watched free scheduled posts beat entire ad
accounts to the first sale on this exact playbook."* *STATED, PERFORMANCE, CLAIMED* — single
operator, no data. Load-bearing for the whole sequence and **provisional until PHC has its
own evidence.** Do not plan against it.

**1.7 The honest caveat he attaches, worth carrying.** [17:10–17:26] *"Organic is not going to
be fast. It's a compounding play and it does build over time."* Stated against his own
commercial interest. The consequence for Augur: **the organic-purchase gate is a slow gate**,
and a candidate sitting in it is not a candidate that failed. Recording that prevents the gate
being read as a kill after two quiet weeks.

## 2. Competitor URL → customer insight

**2.1 The method.** [09:33–10:08] Paste a winning competitor's store URL into an LLM and ask
for **who their customers are, why they are buying, and the feeling they want to have after
buying.** *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.*

**2.2 What it is and is not.** This is a **fast first-pass hypothesis generator**, not
research. What it actually reads is the competitor's *own positioning* — their copy, their
imagery, their claimed audience — so it returns a model of **who that competitor thinks they
are selling to**, which may or may not resemble who actually buys. It is one source, and that
source is a marketer with an interest in appearing to know.

> **Standing caution (PHC synthesis, INFERENCE).** Augur's existing methods work from
> **customer artefacts** — review corpora, unprompted problem mentions, actual conversations.
> This method works from a **seller artefact**. Those are different evidence classes and the
> second is weaker: a competitor's page tells you their hypothesis, not their customer.
>
> Usable as: a cheap starting hypothesis to test against customer artefacts, and a fast read
> on how a category currently positions itself. **Not usable as:** an avatar, or a substitute
> for `augur-doctrine-customer-conversations.md`'s methods. If it ever contradicts a
> review-corpus finding, the review corpus wins — real buyers over a rival's guess.

**2.3 The one input worth carrying downstream.** Of everything he extracts, the item he flags
as most important is **what the customer already bought that didn't work** — *"they're walking
in skeptical and the copy has to answer that."* Augur already treats this as gold
(`augur-doctrine-customer-conversations.md:92`: *"4–5 star reviews that mention a prior failed
solution = purchase-motivation gold"*). **ALREADY COVERED here**; the new half is passing it
into the copy brief, which was routed to Scriptor.

## 3. Verify before crediting — a third case

**Ruled: third instance extending AO-2 (verify before crediting) and AO-3 (read the evidence,
not the narration), both proposed from the 2026-07-29 "One Person Business" intake.**

**3.1 The staged discovery.** Frame-verified at 02:58, and this is the cleanest example yet of
why AO-3 exists:

- The search box does read **"tiktok made me buy it"** — the method is genuine.
- But the **URL bar reads `tiktok.com/search?q=Probioderm%203D%20Lifting%20Cream`** — the
  results actually on screen come from a **product-name query**, not from the phrase search
  being demonstrated.
- The tab bar shows **TeemDrop, Ad Library and multiple product tabs already open.**

The research was completed before recording; the "watch me find this live" sequence is a
re-enactment. **The technique is not invalidated** — it is reproducible and independently
sensible. What is invalidated is treating the on-screen feed as evidence of *how well the
phrase search performs cold*, which is exactly what the framing invites.

**3.2 The brand mismatch — asserted sameness carrying an entire argument.** The TikTok product
is branded **"Probioderm."** The supplier product he presents as *"the exact same cream"* is
branded **"Viareline"** (frame-verified 05:22). White-label distribution of one formulation
under several brand names is common and this may well be the same item — but it is **asserted,
never shown**, and the **entire margin argument depends on it**: if they are different
products, the $9.29-versus-$50 comparison compares two things.

> **The rule this sharpens.** AO-2 says check dates before crediting an originator. This case
> extends it: **check identity before comparing.** A margin, a price comparison, or a
> saturation read is only as good as the claim that two listings are the same product — and
> "same category, similar photo" is not that claim. Named for the file: *sameness is a claim,
> not an observation.*

**3.3 Why both findings needed frames.** Neither is visible in the transcript, where only the
speaker's assertion appears. Third consecutive intake in which frame extraction changed a
finding — recorded as continuing support for AO-4 (the frames-required calibration note).
