## 1. Searching the problem in the customer's own words

**STATED · PERSUASION · ASSERTED** [11:01]. The speaker types the customer's problem as the
customer would say it — *"get baby to sleep"* — into **TikTok search**, and reads the returned
feed for a recurring shape: a parent on camera, the problem stated as lived experience, a
solution promoted, a soft CTA at the end.

The query form is what distinguishes this. It is not a product name, not a trend phrase, and
not a category — it is **the problem, in the phrasing of someone experiencing it**. The result
set is therefore populated by people who chose to speak to that problem, which is why it
returns language and framing rather than listings.

## 2. The competitor-selling-into-the-audience read

**STATED · PERSUASION · ASSERTED** [11:11–11:22]. Clicking through to the creator of the top
result, the speaker observes she is selling her own paid course teaching parents the same
thing — and treats that as confirmation the audience buys solutions to this problem, not just
consumes content about it.

**This is a sophistication-stage and demand signal read off a search-results page**, and it
maps onto Augur's existing `competitor claims` pass (`SKILL.md:51`) — but that pass is
scoped to the **Meta Ad Library and long-running ads**, i.e. to people already paying to
reach the audience. An organic creator monetising the same audience is a different and
cheaper signal: it costs nothing to check, and it distinguishes *"people talk about this"*
from *"people pay to solve this."*

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE), and the caution attached to it.** The strength of this signal
> is that it is a **revealed-preference proxy** — someone is willing to build a paid product
> for this audience. Its weakness is that it evidences the *creator's* belief in demand, not
> demand itself; a creator selling a course proves someone thinks it will sell. Under
> `SKILL.md:124` (*"a validated demand signal is an input to a decision, never the decision"*)
> this is an input, and under the skill's confidence labelling it is **hypothesis-stage, not
> validated**, until real purchase evidence backs it.

## 3. Source-reliability caveat — carry this forward

**Transcript-only extraction with no frames.** The prior Hampton intake
(`augur-video-hampton-zero-dollar-dropship.md:137–149`) established rule **AO-3 — read the
evidence, not the narration** — precisely because a narrated "watch me search this live"
sequence turned out to be a re-enactment with a different query in the URL bar.

**That check could not be run here.** No frames were sampled, so the search shown, the query
actually executed, and the tab state are all unverified. Everything in §1 and §2 is what the
speaker *said he did*. If this method is adopted, adopt it on its own reproducible merits —
which are real, it costs nothing to run and re-run — and **not** on the strength of the
on-screen demonstration, which was not examined.
