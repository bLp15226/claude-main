## 1. The copycat performance spread — a competitive read in one search

**1.1 The method.** [01:47–02:08] Paste a competitor's exact video title into platform search
to surface everyone else attempting the same thing, then read the performance spread across
those attempts. One query returns the field, each entry carrying views, channel size, and
publication date. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.*

**1.2 What the search actually returned.** Frame-verified at 02:01, zoomed to read metadata:

| Title | Views | Channel | Subs | Age |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "10 US Bread Brands to AVOID and 3 That Are Actually Safe" (**verbatim**) | **4** | Consumer Exposed TV | ~6 | 14 hours |
| "10 US Bread Brands to AVOID and 3 That Are Actually Safe" (**verbatim**) | **6.3K** | Preppers Survival | 1.2K | 2 weeks |
| "I Investigated 10 Bread Brands in US (Only 3 Are Family Safe)" (**reworded**) | **~800K** | Disclosure | ~200K+ | 3 months |

**1.3 What it supports, and what it does not.** The spread is consistent with verbatim
duplication underperforming and reworded transplant performing. But **channel authority is
an uncontrolled variable** — the ~800K entry also has roughly two hundred thousand
subscribers against six and twelve hundred. A read like this establishes a *pattern worth
testing*, not a causal claim.

> **Standing caution for Augur (PHC synthesis, INFERENCE).** A one-query competitive read is
> cheap and genuinely informative about *the shape of a field* — how many are attempting it,
> how recently, with what apparent success. It is nearly worthless for attributing cause,
> because everything that differs between those channels differs at once: authority,
> thumbnail, retention, upload history, luck. Report the spread; do not report a mechanism
> the spread cannot isolate.

## 2. Verify before crediting — the method demonstrated by the source's own error

**Ruled: keep as a research-method example. This is the substance of the routing.**

The video presents the modelled channel as a one-month-old originator that copycats are
failing to imitate, and states of the field: "None of these people are getting any views."
**Both claims fail against the same frame the speaker put on screen.**

**2.1 The volume claim is contradicted.** One entry has roughly 800,000 views. "None of
these people are getting any views" is false as stated.

**2.2 The origination claim is unsupported.** The ~800K reworded variant is **three months
old**. The channel presented as the originator is stated to be **one month old**. On the
video's own evidence, the channel it credits with the format may itself be downstream of an
earlier execution. Who originated it is **unverified in both directions** — the point is not
that the speaker is wrong about origination, but that he is *confident* about it while
displaying evidence that undercuts it.

**2.3 Why this is worth keeping as an example rather than a footnote.** The error is not
carelessness about a minor number. It is the **specific failure Augur exists to prevent**: a
narrative was formed first (lone innovator, failing imitators), and the evidence on screen
was read as confirming it rather than checked against it. The dates were visible. The view
counts were visible. Nobody looked, because the story already worked.

> **The rule it yields.** Before crediting a competitor, a channel, a store, or an operator
> as the originator of a pattern, **check dates against the claim.** A "first mover" that
> postdates an existing execution of the same idea is not a first mover, and treating it as
> one propagates a false lineage into every downstream conclusion — including which
> operator's playbook is worth studying at all.
>
> Corollary: when a source displays evidence on screen, **read the evidence, not the
> narration over it.** They diverged twice in fourteen minutes here.

**2.4 Method note — this finding required frames.** Both corrections came from extracting and
zooming a single frame at 02:01; neither is visible in the transcript, where only the
speaker's assertion appears. Recorded as a calibration point for intake work: for
screen-recorded sources, transcript-only extraction would have adopted both errors as stated.

## 3. Reliability caveats on this source

**3.1 Unsupported authority claim.** "I run a venture capital that invests in YouTube
creators so I have access to data that literally no one else has" [00:43–00:50]. The
proprietary data is never cited, shown, or used to support any claim in the video. *ASSERTED
— carries no weight.* Note the shape: an access claim used to license conclusions rather
than to produce evidence.

**3.2 Undisclosed commercial interest.** Solo appears at nearly every step with an
integration depth implying ownership or deep affiliation, never disclosed; the second tool is
gated behind the speaker's referral code; the free community is the funnel and the lead
magnets are its content. Every tool-efficacy and pricing claim carries this conflict.

**3.3 Two genuine calibration signals in his favour.** He names a free substitute at every
paid step, and he revises a vendor's own claim **downward against his interest** — the AI
tool advertises six videos per $99 tier and he says "from my calculations, it actually turns
out to be around four." Both are the opposite of the usual failure mode and should weigh in
his favour on the tool claims specifically.

**3.4 Internally inconsistent figures.** Channel revenue stated as **$30,845/30 days** in the
title but **~$36K** for the same period by his own estimator, unreconciled. Production time
given as "less than 10 minutes," "under an hour," and "a couple of hours or two days" at
different points. *All CLAIMED.* None load-bearing on anything filed.

**3.5 The video does not deliver its stated premise.** It promises at [00:38] to show "how to
beat this new algorithm so you can get views," and at [12:58] says "next, I'll show you how
to get views" — then ends by linking a different video. The entire distribution half is
absent. Recorded because it bears on what a competitive read of *this* source is worth:
the production pipeline is documented, the demand side is not.
