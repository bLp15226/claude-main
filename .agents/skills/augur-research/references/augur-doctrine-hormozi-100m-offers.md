# AUGUR DOCTRINE — $100M OFFERS (HORMOZI, 2021\)

## Master Report IV: Offer Construction Canon

**Provenance:** Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers: How To Make Offers So Good People Feel Stupid Saying No* (Acquisition.com Volume I, 2021). Full-text extraction, July 2026\.

**Primary owner:** Augur (offer evaluation, market scoring, product research). **Secondary:** Scriptor (offer presentation, naming, bonus copy), Quaestor-when-forged (guarantee math, refund economics), Legatus (offer-in-ad framing).

Schwartz tells us the market's awareness/sophistication state; Hormozi tells us what to construct for that market; Halbert/Hopkins/Maslen tell us how to write it. ## 1\. CORE THESIS

An offer is the total trade: what you give, how you get paid, and the terms. It's the first thing a prospect ever interacts with, so it's the highest-leverage object in the business — the same fulfillment presented as a differentiated offer versus a commodity offer produces multiplicatively different economics (his agency case study: 2.5x response × 2.5x close × 4x price \= \~22x cash collected on identical work).

A **Grand Slam Offer** is one that cannot be price-compared: an attractive promotion \+ unmatchable value proposition \+ premium price \+ risk-reversing guarantee \+ a payment model that funds acquisition. The goal is a "category of one" — the prospect's decision becomes *your offer vs. nothing*, not *your offer vs. the cheaper one*.

**Why this matters for PHC:** dropshipping is the maximum-commoditization business model. Every product PHC sells exists elsewhere, cheaper, with faster shipping. The *only* durable answer is the offer layer — bundling, guarantee, naming, brand — because the product itself can never be the moat. This book is effectively a direct counter-doctrine to the commodity trap PHC lives in.

## 2\. MARKET SELECTION (Augur's scoring rubric)

**Hierarchy of leverage:** Starving Crowd (market) \> Offer Strength \> Persuasion Skill. A great market forgives a weak offer; a great offer forgives weak persuasion; nothing forgives a dying market. Rate each great / normal / bad — a "bad" at a higher tier kills the equation regardless of what's below it.

**Four market indicators (score every niche/product against these):**

1. **Massive pain** — desperate need, not mild want. Price ceiling is proportional to pain. "The pain is the pitch": articulate the prospect's pain accurately and they feel understood — persuasion's real goal.  
2. **Purchasing power** — the audience must be able to afford the price that makes the math work (his cautionary tale: great résumé service, unemployed audience).  
3. **Easy to target** — congregated somewhere reachable (groups, lists, interests, channels). Tactical, not theoretical.  
4. **Growing** — tailwind vs. headwind. Newspapers had 3 of 4 and still died.

Evergreen meta-markets: health, wealth, relationships. Niche down within one — specificity multiplies price for near-identical product (his time-management example runs $19 generic → $2K hyper-niched, same core content). Rule of thumb: under \~$10M/yr, narrower makes you more money; broaden only when TAM genuinely caps you.

**Commit to the niche.** Serial niche-hopping resets learning to zero each time. Most "bad markets" are actually un-cracked offers. Fail toward the Grand Slam inside one market rather than sampling ten.

**Augur application:** add these four indicators as a formal scoring block in every product/niche research brief. The Ad Library methodology (mine operator discipline, not category) is compatible — a page showing sustained multi-creative signal is *evidence of indicator strength* in that market.

## 3\. PRICING DOCTRINE

**The commodity problem.** Pricing at/near market \= pricing for market efficiency \= margins compressed to survival. Copying competitors' prices is copying broke people. There's no strategic benefit to being second-cheapest; there is one to being the most expensive.

**Only three growth levers exist:** more customers, higher purchase value, more purchases. (Collapsible to two: more customers, more value per customer.) Every initiative should map to one.

**Price-to-value discrepancy is the whole game.** People buy when perceived value clearly exceeds price. You can widen the gap by cutting price (race to zero, usually wrong) or by raising value then raising price behind it — "money at a discount."

**Virtuous cycle of price.** Raising prices: increases client emotional investment → increases perceived value → improves results (invested clients comply) → attracts better customers → multiplies margin → funds a genuinely better product and experience. Cutting prices runs the same loop in reverse and starves the business of the margin needed to be excellent. Premium pricing framed as a *moral* choice: it's what lets you actually deliver.

**Price literally creates value.** The wine study: identical wine rated by price tier. Priced-higher is *experienced* as better. The goal isn't slightly above market — it's far enough above that the prospect concludes something categorically different is going on.

**PHC harmonization:** the standing 3x-landed-cost rule is a *floor*, not a formula. Hormozi's doctrine says price against the value of the constructed offer (bundle \+ guarantee \+ brand), not against cost. For hero products (VeloCurl, SatinShear), Augur should test whether the offer supports pricing *above* 3x — the black-and-gold luxury positioning demands premium pricing to be coherent. A luxury aesthetic with bargain pricing is a broken signal.

## 4\. THE VALUE EQUATION (the book's central artifact)

Value \= **(Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

Increase the top, shrink the bottom. It's division, not addition, on purpose: drive the bottom toward zero and value approaches infinite — pay, and the result is instantly, effortlessly real. That's the unreachable limit to steer toward.

Key doctrines inside it:

- **Perception is reality.** Actual improvements don't create value until the prospect *perceives* them. The London Underground raised satisfaction more with a countdown map (perception of wait) than faster trains would have. Seek psychological solutions where logical ones are exhausted.  
- **Amateurs max the top side** (bigger claims, more social proof) because it's easy. **The best companies compete on the bottom side** — immediacy and effortlessness (Apple, Amazon, Netflix). Sophisticated markets (Schwartz stage 4–5) are numb to claims; they still feel speed and ease.  
- **Dream outcome** \= channel existing desire, never create it. Status is the master desire underneath most others — frame benefits as how *others will perceive* the buyer.  
- **Likelihood of achievement** \= certainty is a product feature. Communicated via proof, track record, and guarantees. (10,000th patient vs. first patient: same surgery, wildly different price.)  
- **Time delay** \= engineer a fast emotional win immediately post-purchase. Buyers buy the long-term outcome but *stay* because of early milestones.  
- **Effort & sacrifice** \= the liposuction-vs-bootcamp gap. Done-for-you beats done-with-you beats DIY on value, in exactly that order.

**PHC application:** the Value Equation is now the standard four-lens audit for every PDP and email. Physical products win the bottom half almost automatically (a curling tool \= result without salon time/cost/effort) — copy should make that *perceived*, per the PDP scannable format. Likelihood-of-achievement is PHC's hardest lever post-review-purge: without fabricated proof, we substitute demonstration, specificity, mechanism ("reason-why" copy per Hopkins), and guarantee strength. That's the honest path to the same driver.

## 5\. OFFER CONSTRUCTION — THE FIVE-STEP METHOD

1. **Identify the Dream Outcome.** Sell the vacation, not the plane flight. Destination-state, felt and specific.  
2. **List every problem** standing between the prospect and that outcome — before, during, and after product use, in obsessive detail. Each problem manifests as one of four objection types mirroring the value drivers: won't be worth it / won't work for me / too hard / too slow. Expect dozens.  
3. **Convert every problem into a solution statement** (reverse the problem, "how to X without Y"). You will solve *every* problem — one unsolved objection can kill a sale (his eating-out-guide story: one concession template turned a chronic objection into a permanent close).  
4. **Generate delivery vehicles** for each solution — divergent thinking (the brick exercise). Cheat codes: vary the ratio of personal attention (1:1 / small group / one-to-many), effort level (DIY / done-with-you / done-for-you), medium, response speed; run the 10x-price and 1/10th-price thought tests to force range.  
5. **Trim & Stack.** Cut high-cost/low-value, then low-cost/low-value. Keep low-cost/high-value (priority — especially one-to-many assets created once, delivered forever) and high-cost/high-value. Bundle the survivors, name each element with a benefit-forward title, ascribe honest value, and present as a stack whose total dwarfs the price.

Sales↔fulfillment continuum: early on, over-deliver insanely to create flow ("create flow, monetize flow, then add friction"); systematize later without shrinking the promise.

**PHC application:** for physical products the "stack" translates to: hero product \+ digital assets (guides, routines, tutorials — one-to-many, near-zero marginal cost, exactly Hormozi's highest-leverage vehicle class) \+ accessories \+ guarantee \+ post-purchase support. A curling wand becomes a *system*: the tool \+ heat-styling guide \+ damage-prevention routine \+ style library. This is how a TeemDrop commodity exits price comparison. Augur's product briefs should enumerate the problem list (step 2\) per product; Scriptor builds the stack copy from it.

## 6\. SUPPLY, DEMAND, AND DESIRE (strategic layer)

Desire is born of deprivation — satisfy all demand and you extinguish it. Demand is fractal, not linear: roughly a fifth of prospects will pay \~5x, so skimming the top of the demand pyramid at premium prices leaves pent-up demand that compounds the next promotion; selling everyone cheap exhausts the well. Sell fewer units than you could.

**Hormozi Law:** the longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask can be — the longer the runway, the bigger the plane.

**PHC application:** this *is* the Founding 50 logic. Capped founding cohort at launch \= deliberately unmet demand \+ compounding desire for drop two. The Coming Soon nurture sequence is the runway. Structural validation for decisions already made — and doctrine for keeping drops capped and honest (see §9).

## 7\. ENHANCEMENT LAYER

### 7a. Scarcity (quantity limits)

Fear of missing out; loss aversion beats desire for gain. Types: limited units/slots, limited bonuses, never-again. Physical-product form: genuinely limited releases — and *always actually sell out*, then publicize the sell-out (the sell-out is itself social proof and fuels the next drop; Chanel's 1–2-units-per-store discipline). Service forms: total cap, growth-rate cap, cohort cap. His own flag: **"honest scarcity"** — a real capacity/inventory number, disclosed — is the most ethical and still works.

### 7b. Urgency (time limits)

Deadlines drive decisions; a launch's final hours produce the majority of its sales. Four ethical structures: rolling cohorts (real start dates), rolling seasonal promotions (real dated windows, renamed per season), pricing/bonus-based urgency (the *promotion* expires, not the product), exploding opportunity (genuinely decaying arbitrage). Always announce price increases in advance — cleans the pipeline honestly.

### 7c. Bonuses

A bundle presented as stacked bonuses out-values the identical bundle presented flat ("but wait, there's more" survives because it works on measured ROI). Rules: never discount the core offer to close (teaches your prices are negotiable — add bonuses instead, strength not weakness); name every bonus with a benefit; connect each to a specific objection; attach proof and an honestly justified value; tools/checklists/templates out-value trainings (lower effort \= higher value-equation score); solve the *next* problem before they hit it; bonus total should eclipse the core price. Advanced: partner businesses contribute real products/discounts as bonuses (their marketing, your value) — optionally with negotiated commissions back.

### 7d. Guarantees

Risk is the \#1 objection; risk reversal is the \#1 conversion lever (Fladlien: guarantee quality alone can 2–4x conversion). **Do the math, not the feelings:** if sales rise 30% and refunds double from 5%→10%, you still net \+23%. Structure: *If you don't get X in Y time, we Z* — the "or what" gives it teeth.

- **Unconditional** — no-questions refund. Strongest seller; best for lower-ticket B2C; requires excellent fulfillment.  
- **Conditional** — refund/remedy tied to the buyer doing the success-critical actions. Aligns incentives; "better-than-money-back" variants (outsized refund, service-until-outcome, extended service, credit, delayed second payment, ancillary-cost coverage).  
- **Anti-guarantee** — "all sales final" with a genuine damaging-admission reason (easily-copied value). ⚠ see §9.  
- **Implied** — performance/rev-share models; pay only on results. Stackable (unconditional 30-day \+ conditional 90-day). Warning: guarantee-motivated buyers can be the worst customers; a guarantee papering over a weak product just buys refunds.

### 7e. Naming — the M‑A‑G‑I‑C formula

Same offer, new wrapper \= restored response. Components (use 3–5, any order): **M**agnetic reason-why (real occasion/promotion) · **A**vatar called out (hyper-specific) · **G**oal (dream outcome) · **I**nterval (timeframe — mind ad-platform claim rules) · **C**ontainer word (Challenge, Blueprint, System, Reset…). Rhyme and alliteration help; brevity beats completeness; test names against each other. **Fatigue ladder** — refresh in this order, cheapest first: creative → body copy → headline/wrapper → season → duration → free/discount enhancer → monetization structure. Never rebuild the machine when the wrapper is stale. Once an offer monetizes, rinse-and-repeat beats novelty — entrepreneurial ADD goes in the wrapper only.

## 8\. EXECUTION POSTURE

Volume and iteration beat perfectionism: run a hundred offers and you will hit. A failed offer means the *offer* failed, not you — depersonalize, iterate, stay in the niche. Skills compound; the first $100K is the hardest. Conviction is a prerequisite for premium pricing: you earn the right to charge by having delivered enough times that your certainty outweighs their skepticism.

## 9\. PHC ETHICS COMPLIANCE GATE ⚖ (binding — overrides §§1–8)

Standing rules: no fake urgency, no fabricated testimonials, no manufactured social proof, honest reason-why for any deadline or discount. Hormozi is *mostly* compatible — he explicitly champions "honest scarcity" and "real dates" — but several tactics cross PHC lines. Rulings:

**PERMITTED (clean):**

- Value stacking, bonus presentation, benefit naming, MAGIC formula, entire five-step construction method.  
- Honest scarcity: real inventory counts, real capacity, disclosed. Limited drops where the limit is true and we genuinely don't restock that variant.  
- Publicizing a genuine sell-out.  
- Dated promotions with real start/end dates, honestly renamed by season.  
- Promotion-expiry urgency ("the discount ends, the product doesn't") — Hormozi himself frames this as the integrity-preserving version. Reason-why still required per standing rule.  
- Announced price increases.  
- All guarantee types except as flagged below; guarantee stacking; conditional guarantees tied to real success actions.  
- Partner-sourced bonuses at genuinely negotiated values.

**PERMITTED WITH CONDITIONS:**

- Bonus dollar-values: only if each value is honestly derivable (real standalone price, real savings, documented basis). No invented "$1,000 value" tags. This is the optimizer-drift tripwire — external feedback suggesting inflated anchors gets rejected.  
- "Always sell out" discipline: ordering less than expected demand is legitimate merchandising; *claiming* a sell-out that didn't happen is banned.  
- Deliberately capped drops (§6 skim strategy): fine when the cap is real and honored — no reopening "sold out" drops under the same name.

**BANNED for PHC:**

- Arbitrary "fullness" lines ("81% to capacity" where the line is fictional). Hormozi presents this as honest; for PHC it fails the honest-reason-why test unless the capacity number is a real operational constraint.  
- Anti-guarantee with a fabricated reason-why ("just make one up that sounds compelling" — verbatim doctrine violation; a real damaging admission would be acceptable, but PHC's model doesn't produce one).  
- Fake countdown timers, evergreen "closing tonight" loops, phantom "someone dropped out so a spot opened" lines unless literally true.  
- Any bonus-scarcity claim ("only 3 left") that isn't inventory-true.  
- Value-anchoring the stack total against numbers no one ever paid.

**Net ruling:** Hormozi's *frameworks* enter doctrine whole; roughly 15% of his *tactics* are quarantined. The confession brand makes this stricter-than-Hormozi stance a positioning asset: "our deadlines are real" is itself Stage-5 sophistication copy (Schwartz: identification/honesty plays when the market has heard every claim).

## 10\. AUGUR OPERATING CHANGES (do these)

1. **Market scoring block** — every research brief scores Pain / Purchasing Power / Targetability / Growth (1–5 each) plus market\>offer\>skill triage.  
2. **Offer audit rubric** — every product evaluated on the four value drivers \+ "can this be price-compared?" A product that can't be stacked out of commodity status is a cull candidate regardless of margin.  
3. **Problem-list deliverable** — Augur's product briefs enumerate the full step-2 problem list (before/during/after) so Scriptor builds solution-stack copy from research, not imagination.  
4. **Stack requirement** — no rebuilt PDP ships as bare product. Minimum: product \+ ≥2 digital assets \+ guarantee \+ named offer. VeloCurl/SatinShear are the test cases.  
5. **Guarantee design** — pick and math-check a named, teeth-bearing guarantee per hero product (refund-rate breakeven per §7d) before launch. Feed the math to Quaestor when forged.  
6. **Pricing review** — 3x rule reclassified as floor; hero products priced against constructed-offer value, subject to Ben's standing approval requirement for price changes.  
7. **Naming pass** — Founding 50 launch offer and Coming Soon sequence offers get MAGIC-formula names; wrapper-first fatigue ladder adopted for all future promos.
