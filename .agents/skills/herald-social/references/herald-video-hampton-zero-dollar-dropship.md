## 1. Organic consistency without a daily posting habit

**1.1 Home base first.** [16:18–16:35] Create a dedicated page for the brand as the product's
"home base" — name, logo, the identity assets — before any content goes out. Two minutes of
work, and it gives scheduled posts somewhere to live that reads as a brand rather than an
account. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.*

**1.2 The mechanic — batch-load once, schedule daily.** [16:36–17:04] Inside **Meta Business
Suite → Planner**: load **every** creative in one sitting, then schedule posts to go out daily
across the calendar. His framing: *"you're not going to have to wake up every single morning
to post like a maniac. **You just load this thing once and then it runs without you.**"*
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED* — frame-verified at 16:50: Business Suite composer
posting to the brand page, content-manager calendar view in the URL.

> **Coverage checked 2026-07-29** (searched: "business suite", "planner", "schedule post",
> "batch"): **one incidental hit only** — `legatus-meta-ads/SKILL.md:45`, about Business Suite
> *auth*, unrelated. **NEW.**

**1.3 Why this is worth having (PHC synthesis, INFERENCE).** The constraint it solves is the
one that actually kills solo organic: not idea supply, and not quality, but **the daily
transaction cost of publishing.** Herald's three locked pillars can produce plenty of content;
what fails is a human being needing to show up every morning to post it, indefinitely, while
running everything else.

Batching separates **making** from **publishing** so they can happen on different schedules.
The practical consequence for Herald's planning output: a fortnight's content plan should be
produced *as a loadable batch* — every asset finished and ordered, ready to enter a scheduler
in one sitting — rather than as a list of things to make later. A plan that requires fourteen
separate acts of publishing has a much lower chance of surviving week two than the same plan
loaded once.

**1.4 The honest expectation attached.** [17:10–17:26] He states, against his own commercial
interest, that **"organic is not going to be fast. It's a compounding play and it does build
over time."** *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.* Worth keeping alongside §1.2, because
the batching mechanic makes consistency cheap and consistency is exactly what a compounding
play requires — but it does not make the compounding faster, and a scheduler running for two
weeks proves nothing yet.

**1.5 The claim attached to it, held as provisional.** *"I've watched free scheduled posts beat
entire ad accounts to the first sale on this exact playbook."*
*STATED, PERFORMANCE, CLAIMED* — single operator, no data shown. This is the load-bearing
claim under the whole organic-first sequence, and it stays **provisional until PHC has its
own evidence.** Do not repeat it as an expectation to plan against.

**1.6 Where the other half went, and why the split matters.** The principle this mechanic
serves — that a purchase from a free organic post is proof no paid click can supply — was
ruled to **Augur**, as an extension of its existing kill-switch-before-ad-spend doctrine.
Herald owns *how the content ships*; Augur owns *what a resulting sale licenses you to
conclude*. Herald should not be the general that adjudicates whether a product passed
validation.
