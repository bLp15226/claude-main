## 1. De-genericisation by injected source material

**1.1 The practice.** [04:33–04:59] When generating a script, feed the model real outside
material as context — PDFs found online, podcasts on the subject, other videos covering it
— explicitly so that "we're not sharing generic stuff like everyone else." The paired
prompt he demonstrates: *"Find five lesser-known facts and obscure details I can share in
this video."*
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED / the prompt itself DEMONSTRATED.*

**1.2 Why it belongs in Herald's lane.** The mechanic is not about how an asset is made; it
is about **what goes into content before it is drafted**. It answers the question every
organic post faces — why would anyone read this rather than the eleven identical versions
of it — and it answers it with *specificity sourced from outside the model*. That is a
research-into-content habit.

**1.3 The transferable rule (PHC synthesis, INFERENCE).** Stated generally, independent of
tooling: **generic output is a symptom of generic input.** A model given only a topic
returns the consensus summary of that topic, because that is what the topic alone
specifies. The fix is not better prompting — it is supplying material the consensus does
not contain: a specific source, a specific number, a specific document, a specific
first-hand account.

For PHC's three locked pillars this bites hardest on **the Craft**, where the whole value
of a post is detail a competitor cannot restate — construction specifics, supplier
realities, material behaviour. A Craft post assembled from what a model already knows
about the category is precisely the post that reads like everyone else's.

**1.4 What the source does NOT supply, and it matters.** The "obscure facts" prompt returns
claims. The video attaches **no step whatsoever** between receiving those claims and
publishing them — and it publishes them as consumer-safety assertions about named brands.
The technique's output is *unverified by construction*. See §2.

## 2. VERIFICATION GATE — hard precondition, ruled

**Ruled 2026-07-29: a precondition on this chunk, not an enhancement. Applies to §1 in
full.**

The source's pipeline generates the factual content of a consumer-warning video from an AI
prompt with no fact-check step before publication. That is directly against PHC's Third
Law — *verified over plausible; no state-laundering; a guess never gets written in a
confident, declarative sentence.*

Per Axis 1 this is a **process** claim, so neither the book stack nor recent-practitioner
authority governs it. **PHC's own operating rules do, and they win outright.**

> **Binding form for Herald.** Any "lesser-known fact" surfaced by a model is a **lead, not
> a fact.** Before it appears in a PHC post it is either:
>
> - traced to a nameable source that can be checked, or
> - restated as uncertain in the post itself, or
> - dropped.
>
> The interesting-ness of a detail is not evidence for it, and an obscure claim is *harder*
> to verify than a common one — which means this technique raises verification cost at
> exactly the moment it raises the temptation to skip it. **That is the failure mode to
> watch for: the more novel the fact, the stronger the pull to publish it unchecked.**

The technique is adoptable with the gate attached. It is not adoptable without it.
