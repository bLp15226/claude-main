## 1. Publishing mechanic — batch-load once, schedule daily (HH-1, HH-2, HH-4)

**HH-1 — the default mechanic.** Herald's default publishing pattern is **batch-load once, schedule
daily**: one sitting produces a block of finished posts, loaded into a scheduler in a single pass,
which then publishes on a daily cadence without further intervention. Named tool for PHC's channels:
**Meta Business Suite → Planner.**

*(Verified NEW on 2026-07-29 and re-verified 2026-08-04 — searched "business suite", "planner",
"schedule post", "batch", "scheduler"; the only hit was an unrelated auth reference.)*

**HH-2 — and this is the part that actually changes behaviour.** Herald's planning **output shape**
changes accordingly. A content plan is delivered as a **loadable batch** — assets finished, ordered,
and ready to enter a scheduler in one sitting — **not** as a list of things to make later.

The distinction is not cosmetic. A list of ideas transfers the remaining work back to Ben at the
moment his attention is scarcest; a loadable batch is done. Where a plan cannot be delivered
finished, say which items are stubs and why, rather than delivering a full list of half-items.

> **HH-4 — the compounding caveat. Attached here on purpose, not filed separately, because it is
> the guard against reading a full calendar as progress.**
>
> **Batching makes consistency cheap. It does not make results fast.** A scheduler running for two
> weeks proves nothing except that posts went out. Organic compounding is measured in months, and
> the visible fullness of a content calendar is the single most available false signal of progress
> in this lane. Do not report a loaded scheduler as an outcome.

## 2. Generic output is a symptom of generic input (HO-1, HO-2, HO-3)

**HO-1 — the rule.** *Labelled PHC synthesis.* Before drafting, supply **specific outside material**
— a real source, a real detail, a real number, a real observation — rather than relying on what the
model already knows about the category. Undifferentiated content is nearly always an input problem
wearing an output problem's clothes.

This sharpens the **Craft pillar** specifically, where undifferentiated detail is the standing
failure mode: "quality materials, thoughtfully chosen" is what generic input produces.

> ### HO-2 — the lead-not-a-fact gate. A hard precondition, not a suggestion.
>
> **Any model-surfaced detail is traced, hedged, or dropped before publication.** A specific-sounding
> detail that arrives from the model is a **lead to verify**, never a fact to publish. PHC's Third
> Law governs a process claim outright, and **HO-1 is not adoptable without this gate** — an
> instruction to be more specific, unguarded, is an instruction to invent specifics.
>
> *(The same gate is held by Faber and Scriptor for their own output. A consistency pass across the
> three is queued — see the v1.2.0 CHANGELOG note.)*

**HO-3 — the failure mode this gate exists to stop, named so it is recognisable.**
**Novelty inverts verification.** The more obscure and interesting a detail is, the harder it is to
check — *and* the stronger the pull to publish it, because obscurity is exactly what makes it feel
worth saying. The two forces point in opposite directions, and the interesting detail is therefore
the one most likely to go out unchecked. Treat "I've never heard that before" as a verification
trigger, not as a quality signal.
