## Theme 1 — What the feature is (PLATFORM MECHANICS)

**M1 · STATED · DEMONSTRATED per narration.** Meta has added an embedded calendar
booking step to the *Ending* screen of Instant Forms. The prospect completes the
form in-feed, then books a slot without leaving the platform.

**M2 · STATED · DEMONSTRATED per narration.** Launch integrations are Calendly and
HighLevel. HubSpot is expected early August (relayed from Meta, not observed).

**M3 · STATED · ASSERTED.** Contact details captured in the form auto-populate the
booking step — the prospect does not re-key name/email.

**M4 · STATED · ASSERTED.** *The most structurally interesting mechanic here.* If the
prospect fills the form but abandons before booking, the lead is **still captured**.
The funnel degrades to a normal instant-form lead rather than to nothing. Heath
contrasts this with a website booking flow, where a partial completion is lost
without bespoke tooling.

**M5 · STATED · DEMONSTRATED per narration.** Setup path: create a lead campaign →
select the instant form at ad-set level → at ad level, in the form's *Ending*
section → *Additional action* → *Book time* → paste the scheduling link → set CTA.

**M6 · STATED · ASSERTED.** No technical integration or developer work required.

## Theme 4 — Lead quality vs. quantity (PERSUASION PRINCIPLE)

**P1 · STATED · ASSERTED.** Prospects who book a slot show up, answer, and convert at
higher rates than prospects who only submit contact details.

**P2 · STATED · ASSERTED.** Lower friction yields more leads at lower cost per lead.
*Consistent with the book stack's standing friction principle; nothing new.*

**P3 · STATED · ASSERTED.** The structural tradeoff: sending traffic off-platform to a
booking page buys intent at the price of volume and cost; keeping traffic in an
instant form buys volume and cheapness at the price of quality. Heath frames the new
feature as collapsing that tradeoff.

**P4 · STATED · ASSERTED.** *The most transferable idea in the video.* **Sequence
friction — do not launch with it.** Run first with qualification removed to
accumulate conversion volume for the algorithm; add gating only after low-quality
bookings are actually observed. Friction is a response to evidence, not a default.

**P5 · STATED · ASSERTED.** The gating mechanism is honest qualification criteria
(Heath's agency requires a minimum budget to book).

**P6 · INFERENCE (PHC synthesis).** Heath does not say this outright, but his framing
across the video treats the feature as an *incentive-alignment* play: Meta wants
sessions retained on-platform, advertisers want intent, and the feature pays both.
Labeled as our synthesis, not his claim.
