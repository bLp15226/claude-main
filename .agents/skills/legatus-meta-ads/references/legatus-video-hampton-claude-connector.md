## 2 — The early gate (NEW — the one genuinely novel mechanic)

**The observation.** He gates new campaigns at **48 hours** on three metrics: CPM under $45,
add-to-cart rate above 3%, and **ROAS above 0.8**. Clear the gate and the campaign lives; miss it
and something changes. *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · ASSERTED.*

**Why the ROAS figure is the interesting one: 0.8 is deliberately below break-even.**

> **⚠ CORRECTED 2026-07-28 — PHC ALREADY HAD THIS. Proposal H-3 was withdrawn, not applied.**
>
> The original text here claimed "PHC has no equivalent concept, and the gap is real." **That was
> wrong.** `diagnostic-taxonomy.md`'s KPI thresholds block already reads:
> *"ROAS floor before flagging as underperforming: **above 0.8** (early-stage test threshold, not a
> steady-state profitability target)."*
>
> Same number. Same concept. Already explicitly distinguished from steady-state profitability —
> which is precisely the distinction this section was written to introduce. Applying H-3 would have
> added a duplicate threshold.
>
> **What was actually new — and was captured, in lighter form:** the **48-hour** timing. Legatus's
> 7-day cadence takes its first real read at **Day 3, gated on minimum spend** rather than elapsed
> time. Ruled 2026-07-28 to record the distinction rather than adopt the timing: a timing note now
> sits under the KPI thresholds block explaining that Legatus spend-gates **deliberately** — 48
> hours at $20/day and at $200/day are not the same evidence — so that anyone comparing PHC's
> cadence against outside advice sees a considered choice rather than a lag to close.
>
> **The reasoning below remains correct and is worth keeping** as an articulation of *why* the
> existing floor is written the way it is — it explains the parenthetical in a line that would
> otherwise read as an unexplained number.

A campaign at 0.4 ROAS on day two is not failing a profitability test — it has not taken one yet.
It is failing a **survival** test: no evidence of a functioning path to purchase at all. Conflating
the two produces the two classic errors in opposite directions — killing a campaign that was merely
early, or nursing one that never had a pulse. Target ROAS and break-even ROAS both answer "is this
profitable?"; the early-stage floor answers "is this dead on arrival?"

The 0.8 figure itself is asserted with no evidence in this source, and remains a placeholder in
Legatus for the same reason it always was.
