## 2 — Break-even ROAS

**2.1 The formula.** Sale price ÷ (sale price − landed product cost).
Worked: $39.98 ÷ ($39.98 − $12.54) = $39.98 ÷ $27.44 = **1.457**, stated as 1.46.
*STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS · CLAIMED.* **Arithmetic independently re-derived and correct.**

**2.2 CORRECTION — the formula as stated is incomplete. Use 1.54, not 1.46.**

This is the flagged correction per the ruling. His formula nets only COGS + inbound shipping.
It omits:

- **Payment processing** — ~2.9% + $0.30 ≈ **$1.46** on a $39.98 order
- Refunds and chargebacks
- App / platform / subscription cost

Folding in processing alone:

```
true contribution margin = $39.98 − $12.54 − $1.46 = $25.98
true break-even ROAS     = $39.98 ÷ $25.98 = 1.539  →  ~1.54
```

**The gap is 5.6 points of ROAS.** On a product running near break-even that is precisely the
band where an operator believes they are scaling a winner and is in fact scaling a
break-even product. **Any PHC use of this formula includes the fee line.** PHC synthesis —
INFERENCE, not the speaker's claim; he never mentions processing fees.

## 4 — HOOK RATE (Legatus owns this definition)

**Per the 2026-07-28 ruling, hook rate enters the stack as a Legatus-owned shared definition.
Faber reads it rather than restating it — the same arrangement Vigil already has with Legatus's
winner/potential definitions.** Hook rate appeared nowhere in the skill tree before this intake
(grepped 2026-07-28, zero hits).

**4.1 Definition.** Hook rate is the share of impressions that continue watching past the
opening moments of a video ad — the metric Meta exposes for how well a creative's first seconds
hold attention. It is a **creative-quality leading indicator**, not an outcome metric.

**4.2 The observed mechanic.** Two creatives inside the same ad set:

| Creative | Hook rate | Spend | Sales | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early leader | 35% | $9 (stalled) | 1, at ~$1 CPA on day 1 | starved by the algorithm |
| Eventual winner | 75% | $87 | 7 | absorbed nearly all budget, 3.2 ROAS |

Meta consolidated spend onto the higher hook-rate creative and defunded the other. Per the
speaker, 75% is a very high hook rate and 35% is weak. *STATED · PLATFORM MECHANICS ·
DEMONSTRATED PER NARRATION.*

> **Calibration — do NOT set a hook-rate threshold from this source.** Two observations from
> one ad set on one product is not a distribution. 75%-good / 35%-weak is the speaker's
> characterisation, not a PHC-validated band. Hook rate enters as a **metric to start reading**,
> with thresholds to be derived from PHC's own account once there is data. Per Axis 1, a
> creator's numbers never become a PHC threshold.

**4.3 The more valuable corollary — a low-spend outlier is noise, not a winner.**
The starved creative had produced a sale at roughly **$1 CPA on $1 of spend** on day 1 — by far
the best-looking line item in the account that morning. The algorithm defunded it anyway, and
**was right to**: one dollar of spend carries no information. A human reading that dashboard on
day 1 would very plausibly have killed the eventual winner and scaled the outlier.

**This is a direct caution for PHC**, whose low volume makes single-conversion outliers common
and structurally misleading. It pairs with Legatus's existing scale guards, which exist for the
same reason — small numbers break percentage tests. PHC synthesis, INFERENCE, drawn across his
day-1 and day-4 readouts.
