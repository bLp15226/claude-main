## 1. What the source did

**STATED · PERSUASION · ASSERTED** [01:26]. Handed a five-item shortlist by the model —
hormone wellness guide, baby sleep guide, pet behaviour guide, how-to-make-money-online,
astrology dating ritual — the speaker selects on this reasoning, verbatim:

> the lowest hanging fruit here has to be the baby sleep parenting guide, **at least with my
> current skill set**

The question he states he is answering is *"what would be the best to advertise."* The four
rejected candidates are **not** rejected on demand grounds, saturation grounds, margin
grounds, or problem-severity grounds. None of those is raised about any of the five.

**INFERENCE (PHC synthesis), and this is the part worth carrying:** the operative criterion is
narrower than "what I know." It is **proximity to creative the operator can already source**.
He picks the parenting niche because short-form platforms are saturated with parent-and-baby
footage he can obtain — and two of his three launched creatives were in fact taken from that
supply (see the ethics case study in
`../../faber-creative-flywheel/references/faber-video-eclipse-ai-digital-product-72h.md` §3.1).
**The creative-supply decision was made at the idea stage and presented as a niche decision.**
That disguise is what makes it worth naming rather than simply declining.

## 2. Why this is rejected as doctrine

It inverts the three-box test. `SKILL.md:50–59` requires, in order, a real problem and demand
happening *right now* — *"other sellers are moving it at volume currently, not two years ago."*
The video's criterion answers a different question entirely: not *is there demand*, but *can I
produce ads for it cheaply*.

Operator capability is a legitimate input **downstream** of validation — it belongs to
execution risk, not to whether a product deserves a slot. Applied upstream it does real damage:
it systematically selects niches by the availability of borrowable creative, which is both a
poor demand proxy and, in this instance, the direct on-ramp to the copyright problem.

**The five candidates were also never tested against anything.** No supplier cost, no lead
time, no saturation read, no evidence any of the five sells. Under `SKILL.md`'s existing
requirement that every candidate leaves as TEST, KILL, or PARK-with-a-named-missing-input,
this selection would not have produced a verdict at all.

**The closest existing line is the first entry in the Anti-patterns list:**

> Recommending a product because it is **interesting, novel, or personally appealing**.

That is genuinely close — and it does not cover this. **Personal appeal and operational
convenience are different failures.** The existing line catches the scout who picks a product
because he likes it. It does not catch the scout who picks a product because *it will be easy
for him to make ads for it* — a criterion that sounds disciplined, sounds like
self-awareness about capability, and will read as prudence rather than as a shortcut. That is
precisely why it needs naming separately: the existing anti-pattern's framing ("interesting,
novel, appealing") gives no purchase on a justification phrased as competence.

The second existing anti-pattern — *"Treating view counts or likes as demand"* — is adjacent
in spirit (attention mistaken for demand) but addresses a different substitution.

## 4. What is NOT proposed

**The AI-generated shortlist itself is not the problem and is not rejected here.** Asking a
model to enumerate candidate niches is an idea-generation step; nothing in Beowulf's lane
prohibits it, and the file makes no claim either way about its usefulness. The failure is in
**how the shortlist was narrowed**, not in where it came from. Conflating the two would
reject a neutral step along with a bad one.

**No performance claim from this source enters Beowulf in any form.** The reported $75 net
across 72 hours rests on n=8 orders and on creatives PHC could not lawfully have run.
`SKILL.md:42`'s standing position — that vendor-owned and creator-reported revenue figures are
not evidence — governs, and no threshold is derived from this video anywhere.
