## 1. The three-box product test

**1.1 The weighting behind it.** [00:59–01:20] Product choice is *"80% of the game"* — pick
right and content, conversion and marketing all get easier; pick wrong and no amount of store
polish rescues it. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

**1.2 Validation as a stated discipline.** [01:21–01:31] Beginners *"scroll until something
looks cool… get a feeling and then run with it"* — which he names as gambling wearing research
clothes. **"We don't guess. We validate."** *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

**1.3 The three boxes.** A candidate qualifies only on **all three**:

1. **Solves a real problem** — the distinction he draws is **need over want**. Not "a product
   you like." The test in practice: can you name the specific friction removed or outcome
   delivered? "It looks nice" is a miss.
2. **Proven demand right now** — other sellers are moving it **currently**, at volume. Purpose
   is explicitly to *"never reinvent the wheel."* Historical demand is not demand.
3. **Evergreen** — bought *"in October and July"*, because *"the problem that it solves doesn't
   just take a summer off."* Named failure mode: a product that *"prints money for six weeks
   and then ends up just dying off like a Halloween costume in October,"* stranding a whole
   store build with it. **"You're building a store. You're not chasing a moment."**

*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

> **Coverage checked 2026-07-29** (searched "evergreen" across the tree): hits exist in **two
> unrelated senses** — Hormozi's *evergreen meta-markets* (market choice,
> `augur-doctrine-hormozi-100m-offers.md:34`) and *evergreen creative* in retention campaigns
> (`diagnostic-taxonomy.md:207`). **The product-seasonality test is an EXTENSION of adjacent
> doctrine, not a duplicate.**

**1.4 Why this became Beowulf's step 1 (PHC synthesis, INFERENCE).** The three boxes are
ordered by how cheaply they kill. Box 1 is answerable from the product page in seconds; box 2
needs a search; box 3 needs a judgement about the category. Running them in that order means
the cheapest disqualifier fires first, which is the whole value of a gate. Recorded because the
video states the boxes but not the ordering logic.

## 2. Demand evidence from free organic signal

**2.1 The query.** [02:38–03:11] Rather than a paid research tool, search TikTok for the exact
phrase **"TikTok made me buy it."** His framing is the transferable part: this is not a feed of
products someone *predicts* will sell, it is *"a live concession feed"* of people on camera
naming what they already paid for — *"the buying has already happened. You're just reading the
receipts."* *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED* (method shown).

> **Coverage checked** — "tiktok made me buy it" / "made me buy it": **zero hits. NEW.**

**2.2 The three signals, in his order of importance.** [03:11–03:57]

1. **Recency and volume together** — high view counts **from this month**. An old viral video
   is evidence about a past market.
2. **The same product across multiple independent creators** — *"one viral video can easily just
   be luck. But five different people posting the same product and all of them are running up
   numbers. That's not luck. That's a wave of proof."*
3. **Comments, read for purchase intent specifically** — *"where do I get this,"* *"I just
   ordered mine,"* *"I bought this last week and it works."* His image: *"a line of customers
   that are forming in public."* He rates this the most important of the three.

*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED (framework) / partially DEMONSTRATED.*

> **Coverage checked** — "purchase intent": three hits, **all unrelated** (the Zuco buyer-state
> model's "impulse purchase intent"; cart structuring). **Reading purchase-intent language in
> comments as a demand instrument is NEW.**

**2.3 The distinction that makes signal 3 work (PHC synthesis, INFERENCE).** Comments divide
into **praise** and **intent**, and only the second is evidence. "This is gorgeous" is
attention; "where do I get this" is a person mid-purchase-decision, and "I ordered mine" is a
completed transaction reported in public. A comment section can be enthusiastic and contain no
intent at all — which is exactly the case that looks like demand and is not. Beowulf's
integrity rule *purchases over clicks over compliments* is this distinction generalised.

**2.4 Then flip from watching to investigating.** [04:15–04:34] Search the product itself, find
the stores already selling it, and **record their actual selling price** — here, real stores
running it at **$50**. That price is the input to the margin check.
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.*

**2.5 The worked example.** A Korean "3D lifting" neck cream, recurring across multiple creators
at high view counts, with some celebrity attention — *"pouring gasoline on numbers that were
already moving."*

**2.6 RELIABILITY FLAG — the on-screen discovery is staged.** Frame-verified at 02:58:

- The search box does read **"tiktok made me buy it"** — the method is genuine.
- But the **URL bar reads `tiktok.com/search?q=Probioderm%203D%20Lifting%20Cream`** — the
  results displayed come from a **product-name query**, not the phrase search being demonstrated.
- The tab bar shows TeemDrop, Ad Library and multiple product tabs **already open**.

**The technique is not invalidated** — it is reproducible and independently sensible. What is
invalidated is treating the feed shown as evidence of **how well the phrase search performs
cold**, which is what the "watch me find this live" framing invites. Filed at Augur §3 as a
third case under *read the evidence, not the narration.*

**2.7 RELIABILITY FLAG — the two products carry different brand names.** The TikTok product is
branded **"Probioderm"**; the supplier product presented as *"the exact same cream"* is branded
**"Viareline"** (frame-verified 05:22). White-label distribution of one formulation under
several brand names is common and this may well be the same item — but it is **asserted, never
shown**, and **the entire margin argument depends on it.** This is the origin of Beowulf's rule
*sameness is a claim, not an observation* (filed at Augur §3.2 as AH-4).

## 3. Supplier sourcing, landed cost, and lead time

> **⚠ DO NOT RELY ON THIS. UNVERIFIED AGAINST PHC'S OWN TEEMDROP ACCESS.**
> `CLAUDE.md` carries an explicit standing rule: *"Never invent a TeemDrop match, field name, or
> Shopify behavior — say 'unverified' and check."* TeemDrop being **PHC's actual supplier of

**3.2 What the supplier page actually showed.** Frame-verified 05:22 — TeemDrop listing

| Line item | Value |
|---|---|
| Unit price | **$2.29** (MOQ 1) |
| Shipping fee | **$7.00** |
| Tax | $0.00 |

| Estimated processing | 1–3 days for 80% of orders |
| Estimated shipping | **8–15 days** |

*STATED as "$9.29 with shipping included" — accurate as a total. DEMONSTRATED.*

**3.3 The landed-cost split — Beowulf's named check, and the source's blind spot.**

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE).** The landed cost is **75% shipping** ($7.00 of $9.29). A single
> blended number hides that, and the consequence is a different recommendation: the product is
> nearly free and the shipping is close to fixed per shipment, so **the dominant margin lever
> is units per order, not price.** A two-jar order lands near **$11.58**, not $18.58 — every
> incremental unit is almost pure margin.
>
> So on an item shaped like this, a **bundle or free-shipping threshold is worth more than a
> price increase**, and the video never notices because it only ever quotes the total. This is
> why Beowulf's step 3 pulls **three numbers separately** rather than accepting a supplier's
> blended figure.

**3.4 His margin rule, and why PHC's stands instead.** [05:41–05:59] He recommends **2.5x cost
as a beginner's minimum** (*"that's not even the 2.5x return that I recommend anyone starting
out"*) and notes $9.29 → $50 is *"north of five times."* *STATED, PERFORMANCE, ASSERTED.*

> **WITHDRAWN — PHC's existing rule is stricter and richer.** Coverage checked ("2.5x",
> "markup", "times cost", "margin multiple"): `scriptor-doctrine-collier.md:194` already carries
> **"The 3x landed-cost pricing rule"** — with a conditional the video lacks entirely: it
> *"survives price comparison only when the page has changed the unit the customer is
> comparing."*
>
> Ruled 2026-07-29: **do not loosen a standard on outside authority.** Beowulf v1 carries the 3x
> rule and an explicit instruction not to adopt looser outside multiples. Recorded here as a
> second independent practitioner arriving near PHC's number **from below**.

**3.5 One-click listing.** Because the supplier carries the item itself, listing is a single
**"List to Store"** button (frame-verified alongside "Order Sample" and "Pre-order Inventory").
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.*

**3.6 The model, stated plainly.** [04:46–05:06] Customer orders from your store, supplier ships
direct; no inventory up front, no boxes packed. *"Your job is a store in the marketing where the
supplier handles the warehouse."* *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

**3.7 Lead time — recorded here, handed to Centurion.** The page shows **1–3 days processing +

## 4. Ethics and reliability

Screened against PHC standing rules. **This source is notably clean** — no fake scarcity,
fabricated reviews, countdown timers, false brand history, invented price anchors, or competitor
disparagement.

- **AI-generated "creators"** — rejected as doctrine, **already covered** at
  `faber-creative-flywheel/SKILL.md:242`, and already rejected from this speaker's prior video.
  Second instance, corroboration, no new rule. **Precision:** the frames sampled show *Mode:
  "Product Ad"* and a product-only render, and the creative actually posted has no human in it —
  a fake human endorsement is **narrated, not frame-confirmed.** Graded ASSERTED.
- **RISK NOTE — cosmetic efficacy claims, and this one lands in Beowulf's lane directly.** The
  candidate product is an anti-ageing "3D lifting" neck cream; an on-screen tile claims it
  *"tightens your face… making you look younger instantly."* A **regulated claim category**
  (cosmetic-versus-drug boundary, ingredient compliance, substantiation), with a beginner walked
  straight into it and no mention of the exposure.

  > **This is what Beowulf's Integrity rule 7 exists for.** Claim-category risk is a finding
  > about the **durability of the opportunity**, not a footnote — a product whose selling angle
  > requires claims you cannot substantiate is a worse candidate than its margin suggests. It
  > belongs in the scouting verdict, before a page is built on it.

  Third entry on the risk-note pile. **Praetorian trigger ruled NOT met 2026-07-29** — the
  standing rule was same-category recurrence, and the three entries are three distinct harm
  types (IP theft, defamation-adjacent, regulatory). Keep logging individually.
- **Source-reliability caveat, second instance.** Undisclosed affiliate relationships, offset by
  two genuine against-interest disclosures (he admits a recommended tool costs money and gives
  the free path in full; he sets expectations against himself on organic speed). Filed at
  Augur §4.
