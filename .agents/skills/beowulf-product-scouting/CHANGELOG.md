**Added to `SKILL.md` - anti-patterns:**
- **Selecting a candidate by how easily the operator can already produce ads for it (BE-1).** Operator
  capability is an execution-risk input *downstream* of the three-box test, never a substitute for box 2.
  **The tell:** the justification is phrased as competence ("with my current skill set"), so it reads as
  prudence rather than as a shortcut - which is what distinguishes it from the existing
  "interesting / novel / personally appealing" anti-pattern. That existing line was a near miss and gave no
  purchase on a convenience argument dressed as discipline.
- **The causal link to creative sourcing (BE-2).** Producibility correlates with **borrowable-creative
  supply**: niches easy to make ads for are the ones already saturated with other people's footage. In the
  source case the operator selected on this basis and then ran two competitors' videos as his own paid ads,
  watermarks stripped. Selection criterion and sourcing violation are one causal chain, not two
  coincidences. Cross-referenced to the Faber ethics case study.

**Withdrawn before ruling, by the coverage grep:**
- The three-box test, its cheapest-disqualifier-first ordering, and the TEST/KILL/PARK output rule already
  cover this ground and are stricter (`SKILL.md:50-59` + closing anti-pattern). The source satisfies none.
- No performance figure enters in any form - `SKILL.md:42` governs creator-reported revenue. The source's
  $75 net rests on n=8 and on creatives PHC could not lawfully have run.
