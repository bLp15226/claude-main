## 1. Second instance — supplier lead time never reconciled with a customer promise

**1.1 What was on screen.** Frame-verified at 05:22 on the supplier listing (TeemDrop,
"Moisturizing Neck Cream", TD SPU SU00148759):

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Estimated processing time | **1–3 days** for 80% of orders |
| Estimated shipping time | **8–15 days** |
| Total lead time implied | **9–18 days** |

*STATED (on screen), PERFORMANCE, DEMONSTRATED.*

**1.2 The defect is the omission.** Unlike the Zuco source, this video makes **no shipping
claim at all** — there is no misleading promise to catch, because the subject is never raised.
An 18-minute walkthrough takes a beginner from product selection through store build, copy,
creative and publishing without once asking what the storefront should tell a customer about
delivery. The lead-time numbers were on the screen the whole time.

> **Why an omission is worth filing.** A store launched from this walkthrough ships with
> whatever delivery text its theme happened to come with — which is to say, an unexamined
> promise against a 9–18 day reality. The defect surfaces later as late-delivery tickets,
> chargebacks and refund demands, and it will present as a customer-service problem rather
> than as the launch decision it actually was.

**1.3 This is the second independent instance in two days.**

| Source | What was on screen | The gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Zuco** (filed 2026-07-29) | Storefront promised **5–8 business days**; supplier showed **11–14 business days** | An **undeliverable promise** — the two numbers appeared 7 minutes apart and the speaker never noticed |
| **Hampton** (this file) | Supplier showed **1–3 days processing + 8–15 days shipping** | **No promise examined at all** — the subject never comes up |

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE).** Two sources, two failure modes, one missing step: **nobody
> carries the supplier's lead time forward into the storefront's promise.** One got it wrong
> by writing a number the chain could not meet; the other by never writing one. That is the
> argument for **CZ-1 being a checklist item rather than a judgement call** — a step that is
> skipped in two different directions by two competent operators is a step that needs to be
> on a list.
>
> Note also that PHC copy doctrine **already forbids** the Zuco version: the Product Page
> Skeleton's **Redefinition** block (`scriptor/references/PHC_Product_Page_Skeleton.md:129`)
> requires answering *"how long is shipping"* with **"the honest number."** So the copy rule
> exists and the *verification* step behind it does not. Scriptor says be honest; nothing
> currently establishes what the honest number is. **CZ-1 is that missing half.**

**1.4 No new proposal — this strengthens CZ-1 rather than adding to it.** CZ-1 as proposed
already requires validating every stated delivery window against the current supplier's quoted
lead time plus dispatch plus the weekend effect. This instance adds a **second trigger
condition** worth naming inside it: the check fires not only when a delivery window is stated,
but when one **is not** — an absent shipping promise on a dropshipped product is itself the
finding.
