# PHC Product Page Skeleton v0.1
### A Brunson-derived sales-page framework, translated for a physical black-and-gold DTC brand
*Source material: DotCom Secrets (Brunson) + Dan Silvestre summary + competitor teardown (tryglamory.com)*
*Status: skeleton for brainstorming. Not final copy. Fill the variables per product.*

**Voice, locked:** direct but never pushy. We name the pain honestly, then invite her into something rather than selling at her. Keep the bump, keep a real CTA, keep legitimate urgency. Kill fake scarcity, fake price floats, take-away selling, and any line engineered to confuse. The tell: if a sentence would embarrass you to read aloud to a customer's face, it doesn't ship.

---

## PART 0 — The Translation Problem (read this first)

DotCom Secrets was written for **info products**: 90% margin, infinite inventory, a $2,000 backend, and a founder who can afford $150 to acquire a customer. PHC is **physical goods**: 25–45% margin, real COGS, a supplier, and a shipping cost.

So the book splits into three buckets:

| KEEP (works as-is for PHC) | ADAPT (needs a physical-goods rewrite) | KILL (violates "family, not a transaction") |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Customer / Secret Formula | Value Ladder → becomes a **Basket Ladder** | Fake price float ("$497 value, yours for $47") |
| Attractive Character | Free-plus-shipping → becomes a **low-friction hero SKU** | Manufactured scarcity ("gone forever when you leave") |
| Traffic temperature (hot/warm/cold) | The 44-piece Star/Story/Solution letter → compress to ~14 blocks | Take-Away Selling ("doesn't matter to us if you buy") |
| Pre-frame bridge | Big Lie / Conspiracy / Common Enemy → soften to **Honest Enemy** | Conspiracy framing on a candle or a curling iron |
| Order-form bump | OTO → move to **post-purchase page** (thank-you *card* ⚠ not live — Block 17; the post-purchase page carries this alone today), not a hard upsell wall | Buyer's-remorse-blocking language ("your order is not yet complete") |
| Hyperactive-buyer logic | Soap Opera Sequence → 5-email post-purchase onboarding | Fabricated testimonials (already burned this bridge once — never again) |

**The Glamory lesson.** Their page *structure* is worth modeling — hero promise, mechanism explainer, before/after, risk reversal, FAQ, review wall. Their *execution* is the cautionary tale: their Trustpilot is stacked with people saying they were forced through a video before the buy button appeared, and got charged anyway. That is DotCom Secrets tactics driven past the line into a dark pattern. Model the skeleton. Do not model the ethics. Every block below has a **PHC guardrail** for exactly this reason.

## PART 1 — Avatar Foundation (do this ONCE, before any page)

Brunson's Secret Formula, four questions. Answer them for PHC as a whole, then re-answer them *per collection*.

**1. Who is the dream customer?**
Not demographics. Give her a name, a photo pinned somewhere, and:
- What does her home look like at 9pm on a Tuesday?
- What did she almost buy last week and close the tab on? Why?
- What's the sentence she'd say to a friend about the thing she just bought?
- What does she believe about herself that PHC confirms?
- What is she embarrassed to admit she wants?

**2. Where does she congregate?**
Which subreddits, which IG accounts, which TikTok sounds, which Facebook groups, which Pinterest boards. Named. Specific. This is Beowulf's job to populate.

**3. What's the bait?**
For an info product the bait is a free book. For PHC the bait is either (a) a **hero SKU** priced as a no-brainer, or (b) a genuinely useful free thing (the ritual guide, the room-styling PDF, the "how to seal a letter" card). The bait's job is one thing only: convert traffic-you-control into **traffic-you-own** (an email address, or better, a first purchase).

**4. What result are we giving her?**
Not "a curling iron." The result. *She looks in the mirror before she leaves the house and doesn't second-guess.* Price stops mattering when the result is the thing being sold.

> **Deliverable:** one avatar doc per collection (six collections → six avatars). Beauty & Wellness ≠ Fur Babies. Same brand, different woman on the other end of the screen.

## PART 2 — The Attractive Character (do this ONCE)

PHC has an unfair advantage here and isn't using it. Brunson's four elements:

1. **Backstory** — Ben's is already written and it's a *loss-and-redemption* arc, the most powerful storyline in the book: built a store the fast way, filled it with filler and fake reviews, looked at it, and burned it down to 26 real products. That's not a liability. That's the About page and the seed of every product story.
2. **Parables** — the wax seal. The Omaha kitchen table. The 43 deleted products. Short, repeatable, told again and again. ⚠ **Two of these three are currently unusable: the wax seal doesn't exist (packaging not live — Block 17), and the deleted products are gated on the open brand-wide confession question (`SKILL.md`). Don't reach for this list without checking both.**
3. **Character flaws** — Brunson's point: nobody bonds with a perfect founder. The fake-reviews confession *is* the flaw, and confessing it is what makes the 26 credible.
4. **Polarity** — PHC's line in the sand is already there: *family, not a transaction.* The **Honest Enemy** isn't a shadowy cabal. It's the disposable-junk, 1,775-fake-reviews, drop-ship-it-and-run economy. That's a real enemy, and PHC has receipts.

**Identity:** Reluctant Hero is the natural fit. Not "guru who cracked ecommerce." Guy who did it wrong, said so out loud, and is rebuilding in public.

**Storylines available to PHC:** Loss & Redemption (primary), Before & After (per product), Third-Person Testimonial (once real reviews exist), Us vs. Them (light touch).

## PART 3 — The Basket Ladder (PHC's Value Ladder)

Brunson stacks *price*. Physical goods stack **basket size and frequency**.

```
Tier 0 — BAIT          Free/low-friction: email for the styling guide, or the hero SKU
Tier 1 — HERO SKU      The one product that gets the first "yes." $25–45. Highest confidence.
Tier 2 — BUMP          $8–15 add-on at the order form. Complements. One checkbox.
Tier 3 — POST-PURCHASE The "next thing," offered on the thank-you page. Not a wall. Not a countdown.
Tier 4 — BUNDLE/SET    The full ritual. The room. The gift box. $90–150.
Tier 5 — CONTINUITY    (Later.) Refill, seasonal drop, or membership. The $10K/mo-profit lever.
Tier 6 — TOP           (Much later.) The brand itself: broadcast, retail, household name.
```

**Brunson's three legal upsell structures** — every PHC upsell must be one of these:
- **The Next Thing.** She bought the curling iron → she needs the heat protectant, not another iron.
- **Do It Faster.** A tool or accessory that speeds the result. *Never* imply the first purchase is incomplete without it.
- **Need Help?** For PHC: the styling service, the gift-wrapping, the concierge touch.

**Never:** more of the same thing, or a random unrelated product.

## PART 4 — Traffic Temperature → Three Doors to the Same Page

Gene Schwartz's rule, which Brunson quotes and which is the single most useful line in the book:

- **Product-aware (HOT)** → headline starts with the **product**.
- **Desire-aware (WARM)** → headline starts with the **desire**.
- **Problem-aware (COLD)** → headline starts with the **problem**.

Practically, for PHC:

| Temp | Who | Bridge | Lands on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot | Email list, repeat buyers | Just an email + link | Product page, product-first hero |
| Warm | Referred, saw a creator, saw a friend's unboxing | Short endorsement / UGC video | Product page, desire-first hero |
| Cold | Meta ad, TikTok, no idea who PHC is | **Advertorial / presell page** — a separate page | Product page, problem-first hero |

> **Implication for build:** the product page needs a **swappable hero block**. Same page, three hero variants, chosen by traffic source (`?src=` param or separate templates). This is the single highest-leverage technical decision in the whole doc.

## PART 5 — THE SKELETON

**Note (v0.2):** the original 17-block skeleton below still holds, but a cross-read of Breakthrough Advertising produced a tighter **12-block anatomy** that's better engineered for a physical product on a phone screen. It's not a replacement of the thinking — it's the same blocks, re-sequenced and Schwartz-hardened. Two governing decisions come *before* any block gets written:

**Decision 1 — awareness stage of the arriving traffic.** Cold Meta scrollers for a curl tool are solution-aware at best ("I want salon curls without the salon"), often just problem-aware ("my hair fights me every morning"). Retargeted visitors are product-aware. The list is most-aware. One page serves all three *only if* the top meets the coldest visitor and each layer down serves a warmer one — Schwartz's gradualization applied to page architecture.

**Decision 2 — sophistication stage of the category.** Beauty tools, wellness gadgets, viral home goods are stage 3–5: the claims are exhausted. So PHC pages almost never lead with a bare claim ("perfect curls in minutes" is dead on arrival). Lead with **mechanism** (how this one works differently — from real specs, which the First Order rule permits) or, for the most burned-out categories, **identification** (who you are when your home looks like this). Ambient lighting / novelty decor is lower-sophistication — a vivid direct claim + wonder still works there.

### THE 12-BLOCK ANATOMY (v0.2, folded in from the cross-read)

1. **Hook** — headline set by Decisions 1 & 2, sharpened via Schwartz's verbalization patterns (mechanism-in-headline, demonstrated claim, removed limitation, direct address are the four strongest for this catalog). Sub-line carries the one dominant desire. Hero media = product *in action*, not on white.
2. **Promise-in-motion** — short video/GIF of the result happening. Intensification #2 made visual.
3. **Mechanism** — "here's why it works": the physical facts as a logical chain, each sentence following the last, ending at the result feeling inevitable. This is where every claim stays inside manufacturer-spec territory (First Order rule satisfied here).
4. **Day-one** — second-person walkthrough of first use, the verbal test-drive. ⚠ **The old example here — "The box arrives. Matte black, gold crest. You lift the lid…" — is exactly what must NOT be written today: PHC packaging is not live (see Block 17). Day-one currently starts at first *use*, not at the box.** Intensification #3. It doubles as unboxing-experience marketing only once packaging actually ships.
5. **Belief staircase** — claims ordered by gradualization: start from what a skeptic already accepts (verifiable specs, category facts), climb to the fuller promise. Strongest claim lands *here*, mid-page, after preparation — not in the headline.
6. **Redefinition** — the three objections every premium dropship-fulfilled brand carries, answered before they're asked: "is this just the AliExpress version marked up" (curation filter + QC — ⚠ **packaging is NOT available as an answer here until it ships, see Block 17; today the differentiator is the selection standard alone**), "how long is shipping" (the honest number), "why this price" (physical facts as price justification).
7. **Identification** — who this is for, as world-building: black-and-gold, family-not-a-transaction, the home this lives in. No customer quotes until real ones exist; after Founding 50, two or three attributed verbatim reviews live here.
8. **Catalog** — the horizontal sweep: every use, room, occasion, recipient, one line each. Intensification #12, and the natural home for gift-angle copy.
9. **Offer** — price, honestly anchored (real comparisons only). Bundle presented here as the better deal — the ethical AOV raiser.
10. **Guarantee-as-climax** — Schwartz's technique #13: the guarantee restates each core promise, each backed by the refund. Also the strongest logic-buyer close we're allowed.
11. **FAQ** — for the analytical ~30%: shipping, materials, care, returns, fulfillment honesty.
12. **Recap + honest close** — restate what they get. Urgency only if literally true; otherwise the close is confidence, not pressure.

The **envelope/unboxing** (old Block 17) would sit underneath all of this as the physical delivery of Block 4 — the one part a competitor can't clone from the page source. ⚠ **NOT LIVE — available via TeemDrop, deferred pending a proven winner. See Block 17's status note before writing a word of it.**

## PART 5-LEGACY — THE ORIGINAL 17-BLOCK SKELETON

Fourteen blocks. Brunson's 44-piece letter, compressed for a physical product and a phone screen. Each block: **purpose → variable → PHC guardrail.**

---

### ABOVE THE FOLD

**BLOCK 1 — Pattern Interrupt / Hero**
- *Purpose:* stop the scroll in under 1 second.
- *Variable:* `{HERO_LINE}` — three variants (product / desire / problem, per Part 4).
- *Contains:* hero image or 3-second loop, one-line promise, price, ATC button. Buy button visible without scrolling.
- *Guardrail:* the buy button is **always** visible. No forced video. No gated buy button. (This is exactly what Glamory's Trustpilot reviewers are furious about.)

**BLOCK 2 — Core-Desire Question**
- *Purpose:* one line, right under the hero, that names what she actually wants.
- *Variable:* `{DESIRE_QUESTION}` — "What if getting ready took ten minutes instead of forty?"
- *Guardrail:* one question. Not a stack of five.

**BLOCK 3 — Trust Bar**
- Free shipping over $X · 30-day returns · Ships from Omaha · Real reviews only.
- *Guardrail:* "Real reviews only" is a claim PHC can now actually make. Use it. It's the polarity play.

---

### THE STORY (this is what PHC's pages are missing entirely)

**BLOCK 4 — Agitate the Past Failure**
- *Purpose:* she's tried to solve this before. Name it.
- *Variable:* `{PAST_FAILURE}` — the drugstore version that broke in four months.
- *Guardrail:* agitate the *product category's* failure, not her personal inadequacy. Never make her feel small.

**BLOCK 5 — The Big Promise / The One Thing**
- *Purpose:* the single claim the whole page defends. Brunson is right that pages die from claiming everything.
- *Variable:* `{ONE_THING}` — one sentence. If you have three, you have zero.

**BLOCK 6 — The Story**
- *Purpose:* why this product exists in a 26-product catalog that used to have 69.
- *Reality check:* none of the 26 have a product origin story yet. They passed a packaging filter, nothing more. **So Block 6 is currently the curator's story, not the product's.** "There were 69. I deleted 43 of them. Here's the filter. Here's why this one is still standing." That is true, it is differentiated, and no competitor can copy it.
- *Variable:* `{CURATOR_NOTE}` now → upgrades to `{PRODUCT_ORIGIN}` once the founder has actually used it.
- *Guardrail:* it has to be **true.** A product with no story *after* the founder has used it is diagnostic — it may not belong in the catalog.

**BLOCK 7 — The Honest Enemy**
- *Purpose:* Brunson's Conspiracy / Big Lie / Common Enemy, defanged.
- *Variable:* `{ENEMY}` — the disposable-goods economy. The fake-review industrial complex. The brand that treats her like a transaction.
- *Guardrail:* the enemy is an *industry practice*, never a person, never a competitor by name, never a manufactured conspiracy.

---

### THE SOLUTION

**BLOCK 8 — Formal Introduction + Mechanism**
- *Purpose:* here's the thing, here's *why it works.* Glamory does this well — the "how it works" explainer with three labeled mechanisms.
- *Variable:* `{MECHANISM_1..3}` — three specific, checkable reasons.

**BLOCK 9 — "So" Benefits**
- *Purpose:* feature → **so** → outcome. Brunson's format, and it's the cleanest in the book.
- *Variable:* `{FEATURE} , so {OUTCOME}` ×3–4.
- *Example:* "Ceramic barrel heats in 30 seconds, **so** you're out the door before the coffee's cold."

**BLOCK 10 — Before / After**
- *Purpose:* transformation, shown not told.
- *Variable:* `{BEFORE_AFTER_ASSET}` — real photo, real customer, or founder-tested.
- *Guardrail:* real. If PHC doesn't have it, the block stays empty until it does.

**BLOCK 11 — Social Proof**
- *Purpose:* third-person testimonial.
- *Variable:* `{REVIEWS}` from the `phc_review` metaobject → `phc-reviews.liquid`.
- *Reality check:* this block is currently **near-empty** across the catalog, on purpose, because 1,775 fakes got wiped. That was the right call. **Interim substitutes:** founder-tested notes ("I've used this one for four months"), an unboxing video, a "Founding 50" charter offering the first fifty buyers a real discount in exchange for an honest review — including a bad one.

**BLOCK 12 — The Offer Stack**
- *Purpose:* what she gets, laid out.
- *Variable:* product + (bonus?). ⚠ **Packaging and thank-you card are OUT of the stack until they ship — see Block 17.**
- *Guardrail:* **no fake price float**, and **no phantom stack.** The intended stack — product, matte black envelope, wax seal, card — is **not deliverable today**, so the current honest stack is **the product and the guarantee, nothing else.** Listing undelivered packaging as stack value is the same error as inventing a "$180 value," just harder to spot. Don't do either.

**BLOCK 13 — Risk Reversal**
- *Purpose:* remove the last objection.
- *Variable:* `{GUARANTEE}` — named. "The Haven Promise." Thirty days, no seals-intact fine print, no RMA maze.
- *Guardrail:* Glamory's guarantee requires you to have used the product for a month and completed two sessions and gotten an RMA and paid return shipping. That's a guarantee designed not to be used. PHC's has to be one she'd actually be able to use, or don't print it.

**BLOCK 14 — Close + FAQ**
- *Purpose:* recap for skimmers, then handle objections.
- *Variable:* `{FAQ}` ×5–7. Shipping, returns, materials, care, "is this for me."
- *Guardrail:* legitimate urgency only — actual inventory, actual seasonal window, actual ship-by-Christmas date. **Nothing that disappears when she leaves the page.**

---

### POST-PURCHASE (where the money actually is)

**BLOCK 15 — Order-Form Bump**
- Checkbox, $8–15, complementary. One sentence. Brunson's data on this is the least controversial thing in the book: the bump works.
- *Complementary to what — added v2.4.0, source Eclipse 2026-07-20:* **adjacency to the problem, not to the product category.** The test is whether the add-on serves the same occasion or the same bad night the main purchase is being bought to fix — not whether it sits in a related catalog aisle. A product can be a poor category match and a perfect problem match, and the problem match is the one that converts.

**BLOCK 16 — Thank-You Page Offer**
- The "Next Thing," per Part 3. Confirm the decision, tell her *why* it was smart, then offer the complement.
- *Guardrail:* Brunson's script says to keep the loop open with "your order is not yet complete." **Kill that line.** It's engineered confusion. PHC's version: "Your order's in. One thing you might want with it —" Same offer, no manipulation.

## PART 6.5 — The Founder's First Order (blocking prerequisite)

Four blocks in this skeleton (**5, 8, 9, 10**) cannot be written honestly about a product nobody at PHC has held. Writing them anyway is the fake-review failure mode wearing better clothes.

**Before the first page ships:**
1. Order the guinea pig SKU through the store. Pay for it. Ship it to Omaha.
2. Use it for two weeks. Take notes on what surprised you — good and bad.
3. Photograph what actually arrives — the parcel as TeemDrop sends it, the product, the result. **Not the envelope or wax seal; those don't exist yet (Block 17).** Photographing the real parcel is also how you find out whether supplier packaging is itself a problem worth paying TeemDrop to fix.
4. If it's junk: **that's the test working.** $30 to learn it, instead of $500 in ad spend.

What this unlocks:
- Block 6 upgrades from curator's note to a real origin story.
- Blocks 8/9 get mechanism copy that's checkable instead of invented.
- Block 10 gets a real before/after.
- Block 11 gets a founder-tested note while the review wall is still empty.
- The Attractive Character gets its next parable: *I bought it myself before I asked you to.*

**Recommended guinea pig: VeloCurl™.** A sales page needs a problem, and this one has a nameable one (forty minutes every morning; the drugstore iron that died in four months). It demonstrates in a three-second loop, has an obvious bump (heat protectant) and an obvious next-thing, and it's already TeemDrop-matched.

*Gate:* confirm landed margin ≥ ~35% before committing. Under that, it can't survive cold Meta traffic and we pick again.

*Not SkyLume™ first.* It's the most beautiful thing in the catalog and the worst first page — no one has a moon-lamp-shaped pain, so Blocks 4 and 5 come out empty. It's a great **ad** test. It's a bad **page** test. Second in line, not first.

## PART 7 — Honest Note on Sequencing

Brunson's whole model assumes **you already know what sells.** He reverse-engineers a proven funnel, then out-funnels it. PHC's 26 products are, by Ben's own framing, *placeholder inventory, not tested winners.*

So: **do not build 26 sales pages.**

Build this template once. Apply it to **one** product — the one with the best story and the highest confidence. Run traffic. If it doesn't convert, the problem was the product, not the page, and you'll have learned that for $200 instead of 26 pages of wasted evenings.

The template is the asset. The 26 pages are the output. Don't build the output before the template is proven on one.
