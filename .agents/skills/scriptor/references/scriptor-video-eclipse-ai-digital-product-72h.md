## 1. The ad script, as delivered

**STATED · PERSUASION · DEMONSTRATED per narration** [13:10–13:29]. The full ad ran roughly
20 seconds. Reproduced here as beats, not as copy — the phrasing belongs to the source.

1. **Conditional identification.** Opens on a conditional that names the *felt experience*
   rather than the demographic: bedtime framed as clocking into a second job. The viewer
   self-selects by recognising the feeling, not by matching a label.
2. **The scene, carrying a specific hour.** "You worked all day and now it's 7:00 p.m."
3. **The ritual, itemized in three concrete actions** — rocking, feeding, and praying to a
   crib. The third carries the exhaustion and the joke simultaneously.
4. **Mechanism named with a number and a timeframe** — a 14-night plan that teaches the baby
   to fall asleep unaided.
5. **Terms and exit in one breath** — price, instant download, 30-day guarantee, link below,
   then a sign-off that doubles as a benefit restatement ("go to bed").

### 1.1 What this is against our existing structure — checked, not assumed

**The four-part ad check (`SKILL.md:70–98`) already governs this and the beats map onto it
almost cleanly:** beat 1 = step 1 (call out an avatar); beats 2–3 = step 2 (educate the
avatar on the problem); beat 4 = step 3 (mechanism as the unique solution); beat 5 = step 4
(deliver an offer, risk-limiting half). The source proposes no new job and no new ordering.

Related coverage found on the same pass, in different words than the video used:

- **Mechanism carrying a number and a timeframe** — `scriptor-doctrine-consumer-psychology.md:93`:
  specific numbers and timeframes out-stop round claims, *"12 minutes" beats "quickly."*
  Beat 4 is an instance of that rule, not an addition to it.
- **The scene device** — `scriptor-doctrine-collier.md:106` (the mind thinks in pictures; the
  picture built in the reader's imagination beats any drawing) and
  `scriptor-doctrine-consumer-psychology.md:171` (the fixed sequence runs promise → **mental
  movie** → mechanism…). Beat 2–3 is a mental movie.
- **Guarantee stated plainly near the price** — `scriptor-doctrine-consumer-psychology.md:173–174`.
  Beat 5's guarantee placement is already doctrine.
- **Urgency** — nothing to check. The ad carries none, honestly. It satisfies the four-part
  check's step 4 by the risk-limiting half alone, which is exactly the fallback
  `SKILL.md:96–97` specifies when no honest reason-why exists. Worth recording as a clean
  positive instance of that constraint working in the wild.

### 1.2 The one thing that is not covered — compression

What the stack does not currently say anywhere: **the whole four-part check can be executed
in about five sentences, and under 20 seconds it must be.** `SKILL.md:70–76` scopes the check
to ad creative and notes an ad "has to earn the attention first," but gives no guidance on
what happens to the four jobs when the unit is this short — whether any may be dropped,
merged, or compressed. This source demonstrates that none are dropped; each collapses to a
single sentence, and the exit line does double duty (CTA + benefit restatement) rather than
one of the four jobs being sacrificed.

**INFERENCE (PHC synthesis), flagged as such:** the reason it survives compression is that
beat 2's specificity does the work agitation would otherwise need paragraphs for. One
concrete hour and three named actions replace a general complaint. That is our reading, not
the speaker's claim — he never analyses his own script.

## 2. Front-end offer / back-end offer — the language half

**STATED · PERSUASION · ASSERTED** [08:47]. The $24.99 file is framed as the entry that gets
the customer through the door; the post-purchase upsell of physical goods is where margin is
protected. Complements were chosen for adjacency to *the problem the front-end solves*
(a ceiling projector and a white-noise machine against a baby-sleep guide), not to a product
category. One attached across eight orders.

**Coverage check — this is largely already ours, and the placement rule is stricter here.**

- `scriptor-doctrine-consumer-psychology.md:158`: *"Post-purchase upsell lives on the
  thank-you page where the purchase decision is already closed and cannot be destabilized"* —
  and the quantity-discount bump is named as the ONLY permitted pre-purchase branch.
- `scriptor-doctrine-collier.md:145`: the thank-you-page upsell as "the second sale as the
  ultimate minor decision, confidence established, wallet already open."
- `PHC_Product_Page_Skeleton.md:78, 231, 234`: the $8–15 complementary order-form bump, one
  checkbox, one sentence; and the "Next Thing" — confirm the decision, say why it was smart,
  then offer the complement.

**The video's upsell fires post-purchase, which is compatible with our placement rule.** The
margin arithmetic behind it is not Scriptor's and has been held for Quaestor.

The one detail our files do not state in these words: the complements are selected by
**adjacency to the problem, not to the product**. A white-noise machine is not a related
*product* to a PDF — it is a related *solution to the same night*. `PHC_Product_Page_Skeleton.md`
says "complementary" without saying complementary to what.

## 3. Ethics — nothing rejected in this file's material

The three rejected tactics from this source (watermark-stripped competitor creative,
non-consensual likeness synthesis, and selling unread safety-adjacent advice) are all
creative- and product-lane, and the full named case study lives at
`faber-creative-flywheel/references/faber-video-eclipse-ai-digital-product-72h.md` §3. None
of them touches the copy in §1 or the architecture in §2.

**Negative finding, recorded because it is unusual:** this source's ad carries no countdown,
no stock counter, no invented review, and no fake price anchor. It sells on the problem and
the terms. That is worth logging — a source can be badly wrong on sourcing and clean on copy,
and collapsing the two would cost us a usable structure.

**Flag carried forward, unresolved by ruling:** a 30-day guarantee attached to an instantly
downloaded file, never revisited in the video. `scriptor-doctrine-consumer-psychology.md:174`
already holds the governing rule — a guarantee PHC cannot actually service is a fabricated
claim. Parked for whenever refund-policy doctrine is built.
