## §1. The Motivational Bedrock: Hardwired Drives vs. Learned Wants

All persuasion rides on pre-existing desire. Copy never creates desire; it channels it (Schwartz alignment: "copy cannot create desire for a product"). The channel has two layers.

### 1.1 Hardwired drives (biological tier)

Humans share a small set of drives that are not taught, do not vary much by culture, and never go dormant. Different researchers slice them differently; Scriptor's working taxonomy:

1. **Survival & vitality** — staying alive, healthy, energetic; life extension
2. **Food & drink enjoyment** — sensory pleasure of consumption, not mere nutrition
3. **Safety & freedom from fear** — protection from pain, danger, threat
4. **Sexual attraction & companionship** — desirability, intimacy, partnership
5. **Comfort** — ease, absence of friction, pleasant living conditions
6. **Status & winning** — outperforming, ranking above, being envied
7. **Protection of loved ones** — especially children and partners
8. **Social approval** — acceptance, belonging, not being rejected

**Doctrine rule:** Every PHC product page must anchor its primary promise to at least one hardwired drive. If the copy only reaches the learned tier (§1.2), it is under-leveraged — send it back.

**PHC catalog mapping (working, extend as catalog settles):**
- Beauty & Wellness → sexual attraction, status, social approval
- Ambiance & Lighting → comfort, status (the home as displayed self)
- Kitchen & Home → comfort, food enjoyment, protection of loved ones (safety-adjacent products)
- Kids & Baby → protection of loved ones (the single strongest drive in the set — handle with care, see §5.3)
- Fur Babies → protection of loved ones (pets sit in the same neural slot)
- Office & Supplies → status, winning, comfort

### 1.2 Learned wants (secondary tier)

Culturally acquired, strong but negotiable: being informed, curiosity, cleanliness, efficiency, convenience, reliability, expressing taste/beauty, economy/getting a good deal, bargains.

**Doctrine rule:** Learned wants are *support beams*, not foundations. "Save time" (convenience) supports "spend that time with your daughter" (protection/companionship). Copy that leads with the secondary want and never cashes it into a primary drive reads as commodity copy — exactly what a faceless luxury brand cannot afford.

### 1.3 The chain-down technique (means-end laddering)

For any feature, ladder down until you hit bedrock:

> Feature → functional benefit → emotional benefit → hardwired drive

Example (VeloCurl register, illustrative):
> Ceramic tourmaline barrel → curls set in one pass → out the door in 12 minutes, hair that holds till midnight → *she feels watched when she walks in* (attraction + status)

**Doctrine rule:** Scriptor drafts the full ladder in the reasoning block for every product, even though only the middle rungs may appear verbatim in the copy. The bottom rung governs image selection, headline angle, and the closer.

## §2. The Two Roads Into the Mind (Elaboration Likelihood Model)

Academic backbone: persuasion travels one of two routes depending on the reader's motivation and ability to think about the message.

### 2.1 Central route
Reader is engaged, cares about the purchase, has bandwidth. Persuasion happens through **argument quality**: evidence, reasoning, demonstration, specifics. Attitudes formed here are durable — they resist competitor messaging and predict repeat purchase.

### 2.2 Peripheral route
Reader is skimming, low-stakes, distracted. Persuasion happens through **cues**: aesthetics, perceived credibility, likability, mood, sheer repetition. Attitudes formed here are shallow and decay fast.

### 2.3 Doctrine implications for PHC

- **Luxury pricing (3x landed) demands central-route conviction.** Peripheral-route buyers at premium prices become refund requests and one-time customers. The whole PHC model — family, not a transaction — requires durable attitudes, which only the central route builds.
- **Route assignment by asset type:**
  - Product pages, advertorials, Confession email → CENTRAL. Full argument, reason-why, demonstration, specifics.
  - Paid social creative (Legatus assets), organic posts (Herald) → PERIPHERAL ENTRY, central handoff. The ad's job is to earn the click with cues (black-and-gold aesthetic IS a peripheral cue and a legitimate one); the landing page's job is to convert the click with arguments.
- **The awareness model decides where the reader starts; the route model decides how hard you can argue.** Problem-aware traffic arriving from a curiosity ad is peripheral-warm — the page must raise elaboration (make them care) before making the case. Most-aware traffic is already central — get out of the way and give them the offer.

**Doctrine rule:** Every Scriptor deliverable declares its route in the reasoning block. Peripheral-only assets may not carry the purchase ask alone.

## §3. Attention Mechanics (Honest Versions Only)

You cannot persuade a reader you never stopped. Attention devices, filtered:

### 3.1 The self-interest headline
The oldest finding in the canon: headlines promising a benefit to the reader outperform cleverness. The reader's question is always "what's in this for me?" Answer it in the headline or lose them.

**PHC register note:** Luxury restraint does not mean benefit-free headlines. It means the benefit is stated with confidence instead of hype. "Hair that holds till midnight" is luxury. "AMAZING CURLS FAST!!!" is flea market.

### 3.2 Curiosity + benefit hybrid
Pure curiosity headlines get clicks and bounces. Curiosity *bonded to* self-interest gets reads. Formula: withhold the mechanism, never the payoff. ("The reason your curls die by 9pm has nothing to do with your hairspray.")

### 3.3 Specificity as an attention device
Specific numbers, timeframes, and quantities out-stop round claims (Hopkins doctrine, `PHC_Master_Report_Scientific_Advertising.md` — cross-reference, don't duplicate). "12 minutes" beats "quickly." "1,775 deleted reviews" beats "we removed fake reviews" — which is why the Confession works as an attention device, not just a trust device.

### 3.4 News/announcement framing
"New," "now," "introducing," "finally" — legitimate when true. PHC's pre-launch state makes announcement framing honestly available for the entire Coming Soon sequence and launch broadcast. This is a perishable asset; spend it at launch.

### 3.5 Visual attention rules (for Artifex handoff)
- Eyes go to faces, then to where pictured faces are looking. Faceless brand ≠ faceless creative — hands, silhouettes, and gaze-directed composition are available.
- Captions under images are among the most-read text on any page. Every PDP image slot annotation must include a caption that sells, not describes. ("Ceramic barrel, 30-second heat-up" not "Product close-up.")
- Long paragraphs repel on mobile. PDP format protocol already governs this (format follows destination) — this module just supplies the *why*: perceived reading cost is evaluated before reading begins.

## §4. Credibility Architecture (The Faceless Brand Problem)

Persuasion research consistently ranks source credibility among the strongest peripheral cues and a multiplier on central-route arguments. Credibility = perceived **expertise** + perceived **trustworthiness**. PHC's challenge: no founder face, no legacy, dropship catalog. PHC's asset: the Confession.

### 4.1 Trustworthiness through statement-against-interest
The single most powerful trust device in the research is the message that costs the speaker something. Admitting a flaw, a limitation, or a past failure signals honesty more credibly than any claim of honesty could. This is the mechanical reason the Confession story works: "we deleted 1,775 fake reviews and gutted our own catalog" is a statement against interest. The brand paid for its credibility in inventory.

**Doctrine rules:**
- Every product page carries at least one honest limitation or "who this is NOT for" line. (Also a qualification device — see §6.2.)
- The Confession is referenced, not repeated, on PDPs. One line + link. Repetition converts a flex into a plea. Register lock: confession as flex, not apology.

### 4.2 Expertise without a guru
No founder-expert persona available (and none wanted — faceless is strategy). Substitute expertise signals:
- **Demonstrated product knowledge in the copy itself.** Explaining *why* the mechanism works (reason-why doctrine) reads as expertise. The copywriter's command of detail transfers to the brand.
- **Curation as expertise.** "26 products survived from 43" is an expertise claim: we know what's good because we rejected what isn't. The culled catalog is a credential.
- **Precision of language.** Vague copy signals a reseller; exact copy signals a maker's intimacy with the product. Pull specs from TeemDrop listings and verify before publishing — invented specs are fabrication and violate standing rules.

### 4.3 Transfer effects — use with care
Prestige-by-association (imagery, typography, ambient signals of the luxury world) is legitimate peripheral scaffolding — the black-and-gold system does this. PROHIBITED forms of transfer: implying celebrity use, fake "as seen in" press logos, unearned certification marks. If PHC didn't earn the association, it doesn't ship.

## §5. Emotional Mechanics

### 5.1 Mental movies (self-demonstration)
Readers simulate ownership before buying. Copy written in concrete sensory language ("the barrel clicks shut, thirty seconds, warm to the touch") forces the simulation; abstract copy ("high quality and easy to use") does not. The simulation IS the demonstration for products the reader can't hold.

**Doctrine rule:** Every PDP includes at least one usage-moment passage written in second person, present tense. This is the mental-movie slot. It goes above the spec block, never below.

### 5.2 Ego-state morphing (the aspirational self)
Buyers purchase for the person they intend to be, not only the person they are. Luxury lives here almost entirely. The copy addresses the reader's aspirational self as though already real — "your evening routine," not "if you were the kind of person who had an evening routine."

**Ethics note:** Aspiration is honest; false promises are not. "You'll feel put-together" is aspiration. "You'll look ten years younger" is a fabricated claim. The line is whether the product can actually deliver the depicted moment.

### 5.3 Fear appeals — governed use
Protection Motivation Theory: fear appeals work only when all four conditions hold — (1) the threat is genuinely serious, (2) the reader is genuinely susceptible, (3) the recommended action genuinely works, and (4) the reader believes they can perform it. Miss any one and fear backfires into avoidance or resentment.

**PHC doctrine:** Fear is nearly off-limits for a luxury comfort brand and *fully* off-limits in Kids & Baby beyond factual safety information. Manufacturing threat ("your baby's monitor could fail tonight") to sell product violates both the research conditions and PHC ethics. Permitted form: honest problem-agitation of a real, felt frustration the reader already has (dead curls by 9pm is a frustration, not a fear). If the draft's emotional engine is dread, kill the draft.

### 5.4 Loss framing
Losses loom larger than equivalent gains (prospect theory, well-replicated at small-to-moderate effect sizes). "Stop losing your curl by dinner" can outpull "get lasting curl." Legitimate when the loss is real and current. PROHIBITED when the loss is invented ("only 3 left!" when untrue — that's fake scarcity, standing rule violation).

## §6. Commitment & Choice Mechanics

### 6.1 The inertia principle
People default to inaction and to whatever preserves the status quo. Copy must make the next step *smaller* than the reader expected, not bigger. This is why the Coming Soon sequence asks only for presence (open the next email), then identity (Founding 50 membership), then — only at launch — money. Each yes is sized to the relationship.

### 6.2 Qualification (the velvet rope)
Stating who the product is NOT for triggers self-selection and raises perceived value simultaneously. It is also an honesty device (§4.1). Luxury brands qualify; commodity brands beg. One qualification line per PDP, delivered flat, never smug.

### 6.3 Choice architecture
Too many options suppress purchase (choice overload — effect is real, moderated by context). Doctrine: one hero offer per page, one path to it. The quantity-discount bump is the ONLY pre-purchase branch (standing rule). Post-purchase upsell lives on the thank-you page where the purchase decision is already closed and cannot be destabilized.

### 6.4 Reciprocity — honest form
Giving real value first (the Coming Soon sequence's job: story, standards, insider access) creates a genuine felt pull to reciprocate. PROHIBITED form: manufactured gift framing ("we've reserved a spot for you!") when nothing was reserved. The Founding 50 invitation must be an actual limited mechanic with an honest reason-why for its limit, or it doesn't run.

## §7. Structural Mechanics

### 7.1 Length doctrine
The research consensus: more information helps *interested* readers and never converts uninterested ones. Length is not the variable; density of relevant argument is. Central-route assets (PDPs, advertorials, Confession) run long enough to complete the case — laddered benefits, mechanism, proof, qualification, offer, close — and not one paragraph longer. Format follows destination governs presentation of that length (scannable PDP structure on Shopify; letter format in email).

### 7.2 The offer stack order
Fixed sequence for PHC pages: promise → mental movie → mechanism/reason-why → honest proof → qualification → price presented with the value case adjacent (never orphaned) → guarantee → single ask. Price never appears before the value case has run. This is sequencing, not manipulation: it's the order in which a fair evaluation is even possible.

### 7.3 Guarantee doctrine
Risk reversal works because it transfers the inertia cost (§6.1) from buyer to seller. The guarantee must be stated plainly, near the price, with its terms honest and operable. A guarantee PHC cannot actually service is a fabricated claim — Praetorian flags it.

### 7.4 The P.S. and closing mechanics
In letter-format assets (email), the P.S. is read at near-headline rates. It carries either the core promise restated or the single ask — never new arguments. PDPs close with the locked closer where register-appropriate: "We're Premier. Welcome to your Haven."

## §8. Repetition & Familiarity

Mere exposure: familiarity alone breeds mild preference. Implications:
- Retargeting works partly on this mechanism — Legatus creative in a retargeting slot needs consistency (same visual system, same promise) more than novelty.
- Brand codes (black-and-gold, charred parchment, the closer) compound with every exposure. Never redesign per-asset for variety's sake; variety spends the asset familiarity builds. ⚠ **The wax seal was previously listed here as a brand code and has been removed: it is not live** — custom packaging is available via TeemDrop but undiscussed and deferred pending a proven winner (2026-08-05, see `PHC_Product_Page_Skeleton.md` Block 17). A code that only exists in doctrine builds no familiarity, and citing it here is how it leaks into copy as though it ships.
- The ~2,900 existing FB followers have familiarity with the *old* filler-content PHC. The relaunch must overwrite, not extend, that association — expect the first campaigns to under-perform cold benchmarks with this warm-but-wrong audience, and don't let Legatus over-index on them.

## §9. PROHIBITED TECHNIQUES REGISTER (with compliant substitutes)

Classic direct-response canon includes techniques that inherently require deception. They are effective. They are banned. Scriptor treats a request for any of these — from Ben, from ChatGPT feedback, from ad-library "inspiration" — as an optimizer-drift event.

| Classic technique | Why banned | Compliant substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Fake scarcity ("only 3 left") | Fabricated fact | Real limits with stated reason-why (Founding 50 with honest cap rationale) |
| Fake urgency (countdown resets) | Fabricated deadline | Real deadlines with honest reason-why, or no deadline |
| Manufactured bandwagon ("everyone's buying") | Manufactured social proof | Real numbers when they exist and are worth stating; silence when not |
| Fabricated testimonials | Standing rule zero | Earned reviews post-launch; until then, the Confession explains their absence — which converts the gap into proof |
| Fake authority (invented experts, fake press logos) | Fabricated credential | Demonstrated knowledge in copy; curation-as-expertise |
| Manufactured fear (invented threats) | Fails PMT conditions + ethics | Honest agitation of real, current frustrations |
| Decoy pricing with fake anchors (invented "compare at" prices) | Fabricated fact | True anchoring: price presented after value case, quantity-discount as honest volume economics |
| False personalization ("I picked this for you") | Fabricated claim | Real segmentation honestly framed |

**The absence-of-reviews position (pre-launch doctrine):** Until real reviews accumulate, PHC pages carry NO social proof section. The gap is addressed once, by the Confession reference. A luxury brand with no reviews and a stated reason is credible; a luxury brand with suspicious reviews is finished. This is the brand's asymmetric bet — hold it.
