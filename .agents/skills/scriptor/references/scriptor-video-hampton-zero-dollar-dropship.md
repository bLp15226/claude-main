## 1. The store as a closing mechanism

**Ruled to Scriptor, not Centurion: this is a positioning and framing principle, not a
checkout-integrity audit. Centurion's lane is mechanics, not metaphor.**

**1.1 One job.** [06:20–06:39] "Your store has only one job. It's not a portfolio. It's not
an art project. It's a **closer** working every hour of every day, including while you
sleep… either closing deals around the clock or fumbling them around the clock. **There's no
middle.**" *STATED, PERSUASION, ASSERTED.*

The value is as a decision filter rather than a slogan. Applied to any proposed page
element, it produces a usable question: *does this close, or is it here because it looks
like what a store should have?* Category pages, About pages, decorative sections and
credibility furniture all tend to fail that question while feeling mandatory.

**1.2 The three-second bounce.** [06:39–06:59] Perfect content and a great product still
lose if the visitor lands and "just feels that feeling that it's off" — his three named
causes are **slow, cluttered, sketchy** — and then "they are going to be gone in 3 seconds
with their card never leaving their pockets." *STATED, PERSUASION, ASSERTED.*

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE) — the copy-side reading, which is why this sits here and not
> with Centurion.** Two of his three causes (*cluttered*, *sketchy*) are **written**
> problems, not technical ones. Clutter is a copy-volume and hierarchy failure: too many
> competing claims above the fold, no single dominant message. Sketchy is a register
> failure: hedged claims, generic phrasing, absent specifics, the tonal signature of a
> reseller rather than a maker. Only *slow* is Centurion's.
>
> This connects to an existing rule rather than adding one:
> `scriptor-doctrine-consumer-psychology.md:120` already holds that **"vague copy signals a
> reseller; exact copy signals a maker's intimacy with the product."** The three-second
> bounce is that rule's consequence stated as a deadline.

## 2. Copy-brief inputs and drafting discipline

**2.1 The input that carries the brief — what they already bought that didn't work.**
[10:08–10:47] Building the copy prompt, he supplies what the store is, what is being sold,
and everything known about the customer — and singles out one input as the important one:
**what they previously bought that failed**, *"because they're walking in skeptical and the
copy has to answer that."* *STATED, PERSUASION, ASSERTED.*

> **Coverage checked 2026-07-29 — the research half is already ours, the copy half is the
> extension.** `augur-doctrine-customer-conversations.md:92` already treats this as a
> research target: *"4–5 star reviews that mention a prior failed solution =
> purchase-motivation gold."* Augur already knows to *find* it.
>
> **What is new is treating it as a required brief input** — the failed prior solution
> travels from research into the drafting prompt, so the objection is answered by
> construction rather than caught in review. **EXTENSION**, filed as such.

**2.2 The anti-generic instruction, embedded rather than applied afterwards.**
[10:41–10:50] His stated favourite instruction: **"If a line sounds like every other generic
store on the internet, cut it and go again until it gets it right."**
*STATED, PERSUASION, DEMONSTRATED.*

The mechanism worth keeping is the placement. This is a **self-rejection criterion supplied
before generation**, not an editing pass afterwards. It shifts the default from "produce
something acceptable" to "produce something that survives a named test," and the test is one
a model can actually apply to its own output.

**2.3 Never paste raw.** [10:49–10:59] "Every time that it's writing, I'm editing it because
you never want to paste raw. **You want to approve every line.**"
*STATED, PERSUASION, ASSERTED.*

> **Worth recording as corroboration, not as a new rule.** This is an independent
> practitioner arriving at PHC's Third Law from the copy side — verified over plausible, no
> state-laundering. Notable in context: the immediately preceding intake ("One Person
> Business," filed 2026-07-29) published AI-surfaced factual claims about named brands with
> **no** approval step, and its output was the worst ethics failure in three intakes. Same
> tooling, opposite discipline, opposite result. The pair is the argument for the gate.

**2.4 Short copy as respect, not as convenience.** [11:24–11:36] Product-page copy kept
"short but extremely direct," on the reasoning that it "respects people's time because we
know they don't have any." *STATED, PERSUASION, ASSERTED.* Light — recorded because it
frames brevity as a courtesy owed rather than an aesthetic preference, which is the more
useful framing when arguing to cut.

## 3. Naming and identity — and the rule about not caring too much

**3.1 Naming by constrained brief.** [08:37–09:04] Ask for ten candidates against an explicit
constraint set: **short, clean, premium-feeling, easy to say, nothing cute or clever.**
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.* The "nothing cute or clever" constraint is doing
most of the work — it rules out the pun-and-portmanteau space that unbriefed generation
defaults to.

**3.2 The category-adjacency test.** His selection criterion, and the transferable part: he
picks the name that **"sounds like it belongs on a shelf inside of Ulta."** Not "do I like
it" but *would this pass as native to the shelf this product would sit on.* Store named
**Lumiere** (auto-captions garble this as "Louair"/"Lamir"; frame-verified as *Lumiere Skin*
at 16:50). *STATED, PERSUASION, ASSERTED.*

For PHC the equivalent shelf is a premium home-and-beauty aisle, and the test is directly
usable: read a candidate name aloud as if a shop assistant were saying it to a customer.

**3.3 Logo brief.** Simple, clean, **works on a mobile phone.**
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.* The mobile constraint stated up front rather
than discovered later.

**3.4 The anti-perfectionism rule — the most useful line in this section.** [09:21–09:33]
**"Your logo and the name of your store will never be the reason a store wins or dies. So
just pick clean and keep it moving."** Aimed at beginners who "burn 3 months on this type of
decision." *STATED, PERSUASION, ASSERTED.*

> **PHC synthesis (INFERENCE).** This is a **sequencing** rule, not an aesthetic one, and it
> pairs with his weighting of product choice at "80% of the game": identity decisions are
> cheap, reversible, and low-leverage, so the discipline is to time-box them and spend the
> attention on the decisions that are none of those things. Worth stating in that form,
> because "the logo doesn't matter" is false as a flat claim and true as a relative one.
