## 1. The store is a closer (SH-1)

*Extension. `scriptor-doctrine-consumer-psychology.md:120` holds the underlying rule; the deadline
and the element filter are new framings of it.*

**Every page element is asked one question: does this close, or is it here because a store is
supposed to have it?** Elements that exist to fill a template — a stock "Our Story" block, a
decorative badge row, a newsletter box in the buying path — are not neutral. They cost attention
that the closing elements need.

**The clutter reading (SH-1 cont.).** Visual clutter and hedged, vague copy read to a customer as
*reseller*, not as *brand* — the tell is not ugliness, it is the absence of anyone having decided
anything. This ties directly to the existing vague-copy-signals-a-reseller rule; the addition is
that layout carries the same signal as language.

## 2. Failed prior solution as a required copy-brief input (SH-2)

*Extension. `augur-doctrine-customer-conversations.md:92` covers **finding** it — 4–5 star reviews
mentioning a prior failed solution are purchase-motivation gold. **Nothing covered passing it into
the brief.***

**The copy brief carries a named failed prior solution**, so the "I've tried this before and it
didn't work" objection is answered **by construction** rather than bolted on. Where Augur's research
has surfaced one, it is a required brief field; where it hasn't, the brief says so explicitly rather
than leaving the field silently empty.

## 3. Anti-generic self-rejection, supplied before generation (SH-3)

*NEW for the pre-generation placement — searched "generic", "cut it and go again", "approve every
line" on 2026-07-29 and again 2026-08-04, no hits.*

**The self-rejection criterion is supplied as a standing instruction *before* generation, not applied
as a filter after it:** if a line could appear on any competitor's page unchanged, it is generic —
cut it and go again. Placement is the whole point. Given after the fact, it produces a rewrite pass;
given before, it changes what gets written.

**Approve every line (SH-3 cont.)** — the copy-side statement of PHC's Third Law. No line ships
because it was generated and nobody objected. Passive acceptance is not approval.

## 4. Naming brief and the anti-perfectionism sequencing rule (SH-5)

*NEW — searched "store name", "naming brief", "category adjacency", "logo" (hits were all **Logos**,
the rhetorical appeal). No naming doctrine existed. Low value but cheap and genuinely usable.*

**Category-adjacency test for a name:** a name should sit adjacent to its category — evocative
enough to be protectable, suggestive enough that a cold reader can place what it sells. Hopkins's
strongest category, and where PHC's coined marks already sit.

**Anti-perfectionism sequencing — stated as relative leverage, deliberately not as "identity doesn't
matter."** Name, logo and palette are **lower-leverage than offer, page and traffic**, and are
therefore sequenced after them. That is a claim about *order*, not about importance; a brand with a
weak name and a strong offer outperforms the reverse, but neither is disposable.

## 5. Decide the monetisation goal before drafting (SO-1)

*Ruled 2026-07-29 as "a pre-draft decision alongside the awareness/sophistication diagnosis."*
**Filing refined 2026-08-04 on the coverage grep, and re-ruled the same day:** a 13-item pre-write
checklist already exists at `scriptor-doctrine-collier.md:224` governing "before drafting any PHC
letter, email, or PDP." None of its items is the monetisation goal (item 5, *Unit*, is nearest but
asks about pricing against perceived value). **So this is not covered — but it belongs in that
checklist as item 14, not as a freestanding rule.**

> **14. Monetisation goal:** What is this artefact actually for — a sale now, a lead, a list add, a
> repeat purchase? Name it before the first line.

**The failure mode this prevents, named:** the **retrofit**. An artefact drafted without a decided
monetisation goal acquires one at the end, and the retrofitted goal never fits — the piece was built
to do something else and the CTA is bolted to a body that was not shaped for it.

## 6. The AOV threshold mechanism (S-9, S-10)

*Ruled 2026-07-29 as a new paired AOV mechanism. **Downgraded to EXTENSION on the 2026-08-04
coverage grep and re-ruled the same day** — the AOV lane is already populated:
`PHC_Product_Page_Skeleton.md:132` (bundle as "the ethical AOV raiser"), `:161` (**"Free shipping
over $X"** already on the trust bar), `PHC_Traffic_Doctrine.md:135` and `:207` (bump + next-thing +
set as the named bundle math). **The bare threshold already exists.** Claiming NEW here would have
repeated exactly the false-novelty error that triggered the audit.*

**The specific hole this fills:** the existing threshold is a trust-bar line, not a managed
mechanism. Two things are absent —

**6.1 Threshold paired with depth (S-9).** A spend threshold and the depth of what it unlocks are
**one mechanism, not two tactics.** A threshold set too far above current AOV is invisible; one set
below it is free margin given away. It is set relative to real current AOV, and moved when AOV moves.

> **The guardrail, which is what makes it adoptable: the gap must be closable with something the
> customer actually wants.** If reaching the threshold requires adding an item nobody wants, the
> mechanism converts a completed sale into an abandoned cart. A threshold without a genuinely
> desirable item in the gap is a tax on the customer, not an offer.

**6.2 State the gap in the cart drawer (S-10).** Placement rule, frame-verified.
**An unstated threshold cannot motivate.** The remaining amount is shown where the decision is
made — in the cart drawer, at the moment of review — not only on a banner the customer scrolled
past ten minutes ago.

## 7. Purchase-anxiety enumeration (S-1)

*Generalises four category-specific tactics into one transferable rule. Nothing equivalent existed.*

**Named pre-write step for physical-goods pages: list the unknowns a buyer cannot resolve from a
photograph, then answer each one on the page.** How heavy is it. How big is it really, next to
something known. What does the surface feel like. What is it actually made of. What arrives in the
box. How does it behave after a month.

A photograph resolves appearance and nothing else. Every unresolved unknown is a reason to close the
tab, and the enumeration is what stops the page answering only the anxieties the writer happened to
think of.

### 7.1 Fit-and-sizing disclosure (S-2)

*"Size chart" appeared **nowhere** in the skill tree — verified by grep 2026-07-29, re-verified
2026-08-04. Genuine gap.*

**For any sized or fitted item, the page carries a size chart in inches, plus the model's height and
the size worn.** The chart alone is insufficient: a chart answers "what are the dimensions", the
model reference answers "what will it look like on someone my size", and the second is the question
that actually blocks the purchase.

### 7.2 Returns adjacent to the CTA (S-3)

*Mechanic only — the source's rationale is explicitly rejected and recorded as such so it is not
re-derived later.*

**Placement rule: the returns terms sit adjacent to the CTA**, where the risk is felt, not buried in
a policy page or the footer.

> **Rejected rationale, recorded deliberately.** The source's reasoning was that customers who see a
> returns promise at the point of purchase *forget it exists* and therefore return less. That is a
> reason to place it for the seller's benefit rather than the buyer's, and it is refused. **The
> placement is correct for the opposite reason:** risk reversal works where risk is felt, and the
> customer should be able to act on it. Keep the mechanic, reject the intent.

## 8. The creative-complexity heuristic (S-5)

*Explicitly **subordinate** to the sophistication-stage diagnosis — a triage aid feeding it, never
replacing it. Per Axis 1 of the intake protocol, the books govern the principle.*

**Fast upstream shortcut:** the complexity of the creative a category's ads are running is a rough
proxy for how far its sophistication has advanced. Simple direct claims still running suggests an
early stage; elaborate mechanism-led and identification-led work suggests a late one.

It is a **triage aid** — it tells you where to look first. The stage call itself is made by the
diagnosis in `SKILL.md`, on the evidence, and a heuristic reading never overrides it.

## 10. The negative-framing title formula (SO-2)

*Headline mechanics are Scriptor's territory. The guardrail is what makes it adoptable — without it,
this is the source's rejected tactic.*

Four working parts: **a named negative** (the specific thing going wrong) · **a specific subject**
(who it happens to) · **a stake** (what it costs) · **an implied resolution** (that reading resolves it).

> **Substantiation guardrail — hard. If the negative cannot be named and substantiated, the
> structure is unavailable.** Not "soften it" — unavailable. An unsubstantiated negative framing is
> manufactured alarm, which is a banned tactic, and the structure's entire pull comes from the
> reader believing the negative is real. Bound by §9's verification gate.
