## EMAIL 1 — WELCOME

**Fires:** \~1 minute after signup.

**Brief block**

- Goal: set expectations, kill the discount-hunt reflex, open the loop for Email 2\.  
- Reader: cold social subscriber; skeptical, has joined a hundred lists, expects a coupon pop.  
- Awareness: brand-aware, nothing to buy yet. Sophistication: N/A (no claim being made).  
- Steady-state emotion: *"Fine, I'll sign up — where's my 10% off?"* (mild curiosity, armored skepticism)  
- Target emotion: intrigued \+ quietly flattered — *I got in before there was a door.*  
- Promise: being early here will mean something no latecomer can buy.  
- Device: promise \+ open loop (the confession, teased, not told).

**Subject (locked, keep):** You're in. Here's what that actually means. **Preheader:** No discount code. Just a founder, a promise, and a front-row seat.

**Body**

Hi —

You just did something a little strange. You signed up for a store that isn't open.

No discount code is coming. I should say that up front, because I know that's what usually lands in this email, and I know exactly how much those codes are worth — every store hands you one, every store builds the markup back in first. That's not the trade here.

Here's the actual trade. You're on a short list of people who found Premier Haven Co. before the doors opened. When they do open, that list gets things nobody after you will get: first access to everything, membership in the Premier Perks Club, and a shot at something called the Founder's 50 — fifty places that exist exactly once in this brand's life. More on that soon.

But first you should know who you just gave your email to.

In two days I'm going to tell you something most stores would pay to keep buried. It's about 1,775 reviews — and what I did to them.

Watch for it.

We're Premier. Welcome to your Haven.

**PS —** No code, no countdown timer, no "offer expires at midnight." When something here is limited, it'll be because it actually is. That's the whole policy, and the next email is where you'll see why I hold it.

## EMAIL 3 — WHAT WE'RE BUILDING

**Fires:** Day 4\.

**Brief block**

- Goal: convert earned trust into desire — the cull as proof of a standard.  
- Steady-state emotion: respect, plus a fair question — *okay, so what's left?*  
- Target emotion: confidence \+ want. Pride-by-association: *this is the kind of place I buy from.*  
- Promise: everything that survived earned its place — and your home feels it.  
- Device: story of the cull \+ promise; identification (who you are when your home is curated, not cluttered).

**Subject (locked, keep):** 26 products. 43 didn't make it. **Preheader:** The test every product here had to pass.

**Body**

Hi —

Last time I told you what I tore down. Today, what's left standing.

The catalog was 43 products. It's now 26\. Every one of the 43 faced the same three questions:

Would I put this in my own home? Would I give it to family without a flicker of doubt? Does it earn its price on what it *is* — materials, build, the way it works — not on a fake review wall?

Seventeen products couldn't answer. They're gone.

The 26 that could are what Premier Haven Co. actually is: pieces for the home that feel deliberate. Tools that work the way the spec sheet says. Lighting that changes what a room feels like at 9pm. Black and gold, weight in the hand, the kind of thing you notice a guest noticing.

And because I can't show you fake praise, I'll show you the real things instead. Over the coming weeks you'll see exactly what these products are — the materials, the engineering, honest shipping times, all of it — and you'll be able to judge with your own eyes before anyone asks you for a dollar.

One more thing. Out of everyone on this list, fifty people are going to get a different kind of invitation. It only happens once, it can't be repeated, and it's the subject of my next letter.

Two days.

We're Premier. Welcome to your Haven.

**PS —** The 26 didn't just survive a cut — they set the bar for everything that gets added after them. Slower catalog, better shelf. You'll see the difference before you're ever asked to buy.
