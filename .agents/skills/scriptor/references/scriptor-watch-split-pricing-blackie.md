## The observed tactic

The advertiser prices the product at **$29.99 with $9.99 shipping charged separately at
checkout**, for an effective price of **$39.98**. All margin and break-even math in the video is
run on $39.98 — the $29.99 exists only as the number the customer sees before checkout.
*STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE · ASSERTED.*

He is explicit that this is deliberate: he states the price he is "going for" is $29.99, then
immediately notes the $9.99 shipping sits "in the back end," arriving at 39 dollars.

## Why this was routed for ethics review rather than adoption

**It sits close to PHC's standing ban on inflated price anchoring.** Scriptor's banned-tactics
list includes fake price anchoring alongside fake scarcity, fabricated reviews, take-away
selling, and manipulative urgency. Split pricing is not identical to a fake "was $79, now $29.99"
anchor — nothing here is a fabricated former price. But the mechanism has the same shape: **the
number used to win the click is not the number the customer pays**, and the gap is disclosed late
enough to do work.

The distinction that matters, stated plainly:

- **Fake anchoring** invents a reference price that never existed. Straightforwardly deceptive.
- **Split pricing** uses a real price for a real component, but relies on the customer completing
  a mental transaction at $29.99 and only encountering $39.98 once they are invested in checkout.

The second is weaker than the first, which is exactly why it needs a written position rather than
a silent assumption either way.

**Three specific concerns for PHC:**

1. **Brand-ethos collision.** PHC's stated business ethos is premium and family-first —
   "feel like family, not a transaction." A pricing structure whose effectiveness depends on the
   customer discovering a third of the cost late is difficult to reconcile with that, independent
   of whether it is legal or common.
2. **Conversion cost, not just ethics.** Late-surfacing shipping cost is one of the most
   consistently documented causes of checkout abandonment. The tactic may transfer worse than it
   appears — the video offers no data either way, and shows no abandonment metrics at all.
3. **The video provides no evidence it helped.** There is no A/B test, no comparison against flat
   $39.98 pricing, no abandonment rate. The tactic is asserted, never demonstrated. *ASSERTED,
   zero evidence.*

## Position, pending any future ruling

**Not adopted.** If PHC ever revisits split pricing, the condition that would resolve most of the
objection is straightforward: **disclose shipping cost before checkout** — on the product page,
near the price, not at the final step. That preserves whatever genuine benefit exists in the
price presentation while removing the late-discovery mechanism that makes it uncomfortable.

That is a suggestion recorded for the record, **not an approval**. Ben rules if the question ever
becomes live.
