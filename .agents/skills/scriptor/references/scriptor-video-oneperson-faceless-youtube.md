## 1. The monetisation goal is a drafting input, not an afterthought

**1.1 What the source shows.** [05:20–05:35] Before generating a script, the tool asks what
the video is meant to monetise — ad revenue, or a digital product — and produces a
**structurally different script** depending on the answer. The speaker selects ad revenue
for his build and notes that choosing "digital product or PDF" would optimise the script to
sell it instead. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

**1.2 Why it is a Scriptor principle rather than a tool detail.** Ruled to Scriptor as a
strategic copy-planning rule: *decide what the piece is for before drafting it.* Content
built for reach and content built to convert are different artefacts — different structure,
different pacing, different placement of the ask — and the decision belongs **upstream of
the first line**, not applied afterwards as an edit.

**1.3 PHC synthesis (INFERENCE) — the failure mode this prevents.** The expensive error is
not choosing wrong; it is **not choosing**, then trying to retrofit a conversion goal onto a
piece drafted for reach. Retrofitting produces the worst of both: a piece too promotional
to be read freely and too diffuse to convert. Naming the goal first is cheap; converting
the artefact afterwards is not.

This sits comfortably under the existing awareness/sophistication diagnosis rather than
competing with it: sophistication stage tells you *how much* the copy must do; the
monetisation goal tells you *what job* it is doing at all. Both are pre-draft decisions.

## 2. The negative-framing title formula

**2.1 The structure.** Frame-verified across the search results at 02:01 and in the worked
example: **"N [category items] to AVOID and M That Are Actually Safe."** Rendered in this
source as "10 US Bread Brands to AVOID and 3 That Are Actually Safe," transplanted to "10 US
Cat Food Brands to Avoid." *STATED, PERSUASION, DEMONSTRATED.*

Its working parts, separable from this instance:

1. **A specific enumeration** — a number, not "some."
2. **A threat frame** — what to avoid, which recruits loss-aversion rather than aspiration.
3. **A relief valve** — the smaller count of safe options, which converts pure alarm into a
   reason to keep watching. Without it the title is only bad news and offers no resolution.
4. **Category and geography specificity** — "US bread brands," not "bread."

The asymmetry between the two numbers is doing real work: ten bad and three good implies a
field mostly failing, which raises the value of knowing which three.

**2.2 What PHC can and cannot take from it.** The *structure* is legitimate and
transferable — enumerate, name the risk, offer the resolution, be specific about category.
**The instances in this video are not**, because their threat claims are invented (§5.1).
A threat frame is only usable when the threat is real and stateable.

> **PHC guardrail.** A negative-framing headline commits the body copy to substantiating
> the negative. If the piece cannot name what is actually wrong with the things it says to
> avoid, the structure is unavailable — not softened, unavailable.

**2.3 Placement note.** Whether this adds to Scriptor's existing headline doctrine or merely
instantiates it needs checking against `scriptor-doctrine-karbo.md` §2.5 (one-specific-promise,
love-letter structure) and the Product Page Skeleton's headline blocks before anything is
promoted. Flagged, not assumed — see proposal SO-2.

## 5. ETHICS FILTER — copy-side rejections

All rejections approved 2026-07-29, including both keep-mechanic / reject-execution splits.

**5.1 Fabricated product-safety claim. REJECTED — most serious item.** Frame-verified at
11:03: a generated thumbnail reading **"POISON IN CAT FOOD"** in large yellow type over a
photorealistic image of a spilled can with the **Fancy Feast brand name and trade dress
clearly legible**. The claim is invented for click-through; nothing substantiates it.

Product-disparagement and defamation exposure, plus platform policy risk on safety
misinformation. **This is the copy-side reason §2's threat frame carries a hard guardrail:**
the structure and this execution are separable, and only the structure survives.

**5.2 Selling one-click unverified AI output as a paid guide. REJECTED as described.** The
product builder takes a niche string plus a channel URL and returns outline, full PDF, and
sales-page copy from one click. Charging $47 for guidance with no subject-matter expertise
or verification behind it is incompatible with "feel like family, not a transaction."

> **Split, as ruled:** the mechanic — *attach a paid product to an audience that already
> exists* — is sound and kept. The generation path that produced this one is rejected.

**5.3 VERIFICATION GATE — hard precondition, applies retroactively.** Ruled a standing
precondition across this intake. The source generates the factual content of a
consumer-safety video from an AI prompt with no fact-check step. Against PHC's Third Law
(verified over plausible; no state-laundering). This is a **process** claim, so PHC's own
operating rules govern — not the book stack, not recent-practitioner authority — and they
win outright.

> **Binding on Scriptor:** any copy asserting a checkable fact about a product, a
> competitor, or a health/safety matter passes verification before it ships. This binds §2
> in particular, because a threat-framed headline is a factual assertion by construction.

**5.4 Source-reliability caveat.** Undisclosed commercial interest throughout — Solo
integrated at nearly every step with ownership implied, the second tool gated behind the
speaker's referral code, the community as funnel. Partly offset by naming free substitutes
at every paid step and revising a vendor claim downward against his own interest.
