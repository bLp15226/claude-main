## 1 — The template

Three beats, roughly 2–3 seconds each before the product appears. Built in CapCut; he produces
five variants per test.

**Beat 1 — a broad, unresolved opening statement (0–3s).**
A first-person line that raises a question it does not answer. His stated rationale, which is the
useful part: it is *deliberately very broad*, so almost anyone reading it wants to know what
happened — the statement provokes questions (what happened? why? to whom?) and the viewer stays to
resolve them. Breadth is the design goal, not specificity.
*STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE · ASSERTED.*

**Beat 2 — the relatability beat (3–6s).**
A second person in a comparable situation, narrowing the audience from "anyone who was curious"
to "the specific person this is for." The function is identification: the viewer moves from
watching a story to recognising themselves in it.
*STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE · ASSERTED.*

**Beat 3 — the product enters as the resolution.**
Introduced as the answer to the emotional state beats 1–2 created, not as a feature list.
*STATED · PERSUASION PRINCIPLE · ASSERTED.*

**The load-bearing idea:** curiosity gap → identification → solution. Beat 1 buys attention
broadly, Beat 2 spends it on the right person, Beat 3 converts the state into a product. This is
orthodox direct response applied to a 3-second-attention format, and it survives the ethics
filter intact as a *shape*.

## 2 — What fills the beats (this is where the rule bites)

The template is content-neutral. PHC's constraint is on what goes into it. Legitimate fills:

- **Real founder voice** — Ben's own genuine experience or perspective, in his own words.
- **Real customer language** — verbatim, attributed, once Founding 50 produces it (Scriptor rule #1
  governs the inventory question).
- **Non-testimonial framing** — a question, an observation, a problem statement, a demonstration.
  Beat 1 does not have to be a personal anecdote to be broad and unresolved; that was this
  advertiser's choice, not a requirement of the structure.

Refused fills, per Faber's standing rule: any invented personal experience, invented event,
invented witness, or AI-generated person delivering any of the above.
