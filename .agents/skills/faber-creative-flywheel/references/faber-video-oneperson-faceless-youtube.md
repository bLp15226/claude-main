## 1. Niche quilting — and the first hard evidence for a rule Faber already holds

**1.1 The technique.** [02:09–02:52] Hold the format, transplant the market. Take a proven
title structure and move it to a different subject. The chain the speaker cites: a birds
channel → skeletons → insects → fish → cats, each operator keeping the structure and
changing the subject. His worked example: "10 US Bread Brands to Avoid" → "10 US Cat Food
Brands to Avoid," with the market chosen because he had separately observed cat content
performing. His coinage, dated to February 2025. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

**1.2 The evidence — and a correction to the speaker's own claim.** [01:47–02:08] He pastes
the original title into search to display a field of word-for-word copies and states:
"None of these people are getting any views."

Frame-verified at 02:01, zoomed to read the metadata:

| Title | Views | Channel | Subs | Age |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "10 US Bread Brands to AVOID and 3 That Are Actually Safe" (**verbatim**) | **4** | Consumer Exposed TV | ~6 | 14 hours |
| "10 US Bread Brands to AVOID and 3 That Are Actually Safe" (**verbatim**) | **6.3K** | Preppers Survival | 1.2K | 2 weeks |
| "I Investigated 10 Bread Brands in US (Only 3 Are Family Safe)" (**reworded**) | **~800K** | Disclosure | ~200K+ | 3 months |

*DEMONSTRATED.*

> **The claim as stated is false; the corrected version is stronger.** One entry has
> roughly 800,000 views, so "none of these people are getting any views" is contradicted
> by his own screen. But the successful entry is the one that **reworded** the title,
> while both verbatim copies underperformed badly. So the corrected finding supports the
> prescription *better* than the original claim did: **transplant-and-reword performs;
> verbatim duplication does not.** Recorded this way deliberately — the correction is
> what makes it usable.
>
> Caveat on causation: the ~800K channel also has ~200K+ subscribers against 6 and 1.2K,
> so channel authority is an uncontrolled variable. The spread is consistent with the
> conclusion but does not isolate it.

**1.3 Why this matters to Faber specifically — third instance, first with data.**
Verified against Faber's `SKILL.md` (rule promoted at v1.2.0, 2026-07-28) and
`faber-video-karolis-ai-ugc.md` §6.3: *"Analyse structure. Never replicate a specific
creator's execution beat-for-beat."*

Niche quilting is an independent third arrival at that rule, and the first source to
bring **frame-verified performance data** rather than assertion. The two prior instances
(Karolis, Hampton) established the pattern; this one supplies evidence that the replica
half actually fails in market. That strengthens the existing rule's evidentiary base
without changing its content.

**1.4 And the same video violates the rule at a different layer.** See §3.2. Its
title-level advice complies; its thumbnail-level advice does not. The line that falls out
of the contrast is worth stating as doctrine in its own right:

> **Compose from the pattern. Do not edit the artefact.**
>
> Rebuilding a similar structure from scratch is analysis. Opening someone's file and
> changing it is replication — and it carries their copyright with it.

## 2. Style transfer by reference, not topic theft

[05:02–05:19] When generating a script, supply the **URL of a proven video** as a *style*
reference — "it will model their style of writing" — while the topic remains your own.
*STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, ASSERTED.*

This is the cleanest articulation in the source of the distinction Faber's rule turns on:
**borrow register and structure, not subject matter.** It is also the operationally useful
form, because it separates the two things a creator is tempted to copy at once — how a
thing is said, and what it is about — and takes only the first.

**PHC synthesis (INFERENCE).** Faber's existing rule is stated as a prohibition (never
replicate beat-for-beat). This gives it a positive counterpart: *the permitted move is
style-and-structure transfer onto your own subject.* A rule that only forbids leaves the
operator without a next action; pairing the two makes it usable in a brief. That framing
is ours, not the speaker's.

## 3. Thumbnail composition transfer

**3.1 The criterion that travels — recognisability.** [10:32–11:24] Keep a proven
thumbnail's composition and swap the subject for the equivalent object in your niche. The
selection criterion for the replacement is **instant recognisability to the target
viewer**, which he establishes by asking an LLM which brand in the category viewers would
recognise on sight. *STATED, PLATFORM MECHANICS, DEMONSTRATED.*

The criterion is the substance. A borrowed composition works because the viewer parses it
in under a second; substituting an object the audience does not immediately recognise
destroys exactly the property that made the composition effective. Stated as a brief
rule: **when a composition depends on recognition, the swapped element must be the most
recognisable member of its category, not merely a correct one.**

**3.2 The executed example is rejected on two independent grounds.** Ruled: criterion only,
not the execution.

1. **It edits the competitor's actual file.** The method downloads the original thumbnail
   via a "thumbnail grabber" and edits that image. Copyright problem, and a direct
   violation of Faber's pattern-not-replica rule (§1.4).
2. **The text it produces is a fabricated safety claim.** See §4.1.

Only the recognisability criterion enters Faber doctrine. The workflow that produced it
does not.

## 4. ETHICS FILTER — asset-side rejections

All rejections from this source approved 2026-07-29, including both keep-mechanic /
reject-execution splits.

**4.1 Fabricated product-safety claim. REJECTED — most serious item in the source.**
Frame-verified at 11:03: the generated thumbnail reads **"POISON IN CAT FOOD"** in large
yellow type over a photorealistic image of a spilled can with the **Fancy Feast brand name
and trade dress clearly legible**. The claim is invented for click-through. Nothing in the
video substantiates it, and the "research" behind the video is an unverified AI prompt
(§4.3).

This is a fabricated factual claim of consumer harm against an identifiable commercial
product: product-disparagement and defamation exposure, plus platform policy risk on
safety misinformation. **Not adoptable at any scale, in any niche, for any product.**
Faber's existing no-fabricated-proof rule already covers the principle; this is a second
corroborating instance in a new form — fabricated *harm* claims about a third party rather
than fabricated *proof* about oneself.

**4.2 Editing a competitor's downloaded thumbnail image. REJECTED.** See §3.2(1).

**4.3 VERIFICATION GATE — hard precondition on anything from this source.**
Ruled a standing precondition, not a suggestion. The source's pipeline generates the
factual content of a consumer-safety video from an AI prompt with **no fact-check step**
before publication. That is directly against PHC's Third Law (verified over plausible; no
state-laundering).

> **Binding on Faber:** any Faber output that asserts a checkable fact about a product,
> a competitor, or a health/safety matter passes a verification step before it ships.
> Generated-and-plausible is not shippable. This applies to §1–3 wherever their output
> would carry a factual claim.

**4.4 Source-reliability caveat.** Solo appears at nearly every step with an integration
depth implying ownership or deep affiliation, never disclosed; the second tool is gated
behind the speaker's referral code; the community is the funnel. Every tool-efficacy and
pricing claim from this source carries that conflict. Partly offset by two genuine
calibration signals: he names a free substitute at every paid step, and he revises a
vendor's own claim **downward** against his interest (tool advertises 6 videos per $99
tier; he calculates 4).

**4.5 Reliability flag on the central case study.** The video presents the modelled channel
as a 1-month-old originator that copycats are failing to copy. But the ~800K reworded
variant in §1.2 is **3 months old**, predating it. Origination is unverified in both
directions; the confident attribution in the video is unsupported by its own screen. Noted
because §1's technique is derived from that narrative.
